### [1] [Permutation theory governs long-term dynamics of critical Boolean networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07790)
*Venkata Sai Narayana Bavisetty,Matthew Wheeler,Julian Vignes,Matthew Stephen Jones,Ruodan Liu,Sean Campbell,Claus Kadelka*

Main category: q-bio.MN

TL;DR: This paper analyzes attractor lengths in critical Boolean networks with connectivity K=1, showing that the attractor structure is encoded by a permutation induced by the network's feedback loops. Using results from Erdős–Turán and Landau's theorem, they prove that almost all networks have attractors of length at most exp[½ ln² N], while rare networks can support attractors as long as exp[√(N ln N)], with the mean attractor length scaling as exp[N^{1/3}] due to the dominance of rare networks with exceptionally long attractors.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: While the number of attractors in Boolean networks has been recently resolved, the distribution of attractor lengths and its dependence on network architecture (topology) remains poorly understood. This paper aims to fill this gap for the specific case of critical Boolean networks with connectivity K=1.

Method: The authors show that the attractor structure of critical Boolean networks with K=1 is encoded by a permutation induced by the network's feedback loops. This recasts questions about attractor lengths as questions about permutations and their arithmetic properties. They then apply results from Erdős and Turán (on permutation statistics) together with Landau's theorem (on the maximal order of elements in the symmetric group) to derive bounds on attractor lengths.

Result: Almost all networks have attractors of length at most exp[½ ln² N], while some networks support attractors as long as exp[√(N ln N)]. Abstract: Boolean networks are widely used to model gene regulatory dynamics, where long-term behavior is organized by attractors - by both their number and their lengths. The number of attractors was resolved recently, but the distribution of attractor lengths and its dependence on network architecture remain poorly understood. We address this for critical Boolean networks with connectivity $K=1$. We show that the attractor structure of these networks is encoded by a permutation induced by the network's feedback loops, thereby recasting questions about attractor lengths as questions about permutations and their arithmetic properties. In particular, the maximum attractor length is determined by the order of the induced permutation. Using results of Erdős and Turán together with a classical theorem of Landau, we show that almost all networks have attractors of length at most $\exp[\tfrac{1}{2}\ln^2 N]$, while some networks support attractors as long as $\exp[\sqrt{N\ln N}]$. The mean attractor length scales as $\exp[N^{1/3}]$, reflecting the influence of rare networks whose exceptionally long attractors dominate the expectation value. These results establish a direct connection between Boolean network dynamics, combinatorics, and number theory, and identify the permutation induced by the feedback loops as a central dynamical invariant governing attractor lengths in critical Boolean networks.

### [2] [MAPK Pathway Activity and Heme Biosynthesis Gene Expression in IDH-Wildtype Glioblastoma: A Purity-Adjusted, Discovery-Validation Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07865)
*Manveer Singh Tib*

Main category: q-bio.GN

TL;DR: This study provides the first replicated human-tissue evidence that MAPK pathway activation is negatively associated with PPOX (the enzyme synthesizing protoporphyrin IX) in IDH-wildtype glioblastoma, suggesting MAPK regulates PpIX at the synthesis step rather than the clearance step implied by prior cell-line work. Differential MAPK activation was seen across molecular subtypes, but no prognostic correlation was found.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 5-ALA-based fluorescence resection of glioblastoma relies on PpIX accumulation, but fluorescence intensity is highly heterogeneous, limiting tumor margin distinction. Prior cell-line studies suggested MAPK pathway stimulation reduces PpIX via increased ABCB1 efflux and FECH clearance, yet this mechanism had never been validated in human tissue.

Method: Two independent purity-adjusted cohorts of primary IDH-wildtype glioblastoma were analyzed (TCGA-GBM discovery, n=140; CGGA validation, n=87). Spearman correlations between MAPK activity and proposed downstream effectors (ABCB1, FECH, PPOX) were assessed with bootstrap confidence intervals, permutation testing, and alternative purity-adjustment approaches.

Result: No reproducible correlation was found between MAPK signaling and ABCB1/FECH. Instead, MAPK activity showed a reproducible negative correlation with PPOX in both cohorts (TCGA: rho=-0.40, p<0.001; CGGA: rho=-0.28, p=0.034), robust to permutation and purity-adjustment methods. MAPK activation was higher in classical and lower in proneural subtypes, with no correlation to overall survival (log-rank p=0.73).

Conclusion: MAPK regulation of PpIX in human glioblastoma likely acts at the synthesis step via PPOX rather than the clearance steps implied by cell culture. This association is biological, not prognostic, positioning the MAPK pathway as a candidate therapeutic handle to enhance 5-ALA fluorescence for improved surgical resection.

Abstract: 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) promotes fluorescence-based resection of glioblastoma via protoporphyrin IX (PpIX); however, there is considerable heterogeneity in fluorescence intensity, limiting margin distinction. Using two independent, purity-adjusted sets of primary IDH-wildtype glioblastoma specimens (TCGA-GBM, n=140, discovery; CGGA, n=87, validation), no reproducible correlation between MAPK signaling and its proposed downstream effectors (ABCB1 and FECH) could be detected. If this correlation reflects causality, MAPK regulation of PpIX would act at the point of synthesis rather than at the clearance steps implied by previous cell-culture studies. We observed differential activation of the MAPK pathway with increased activation in the classical subtype and decreased activation in the proneural subtype. There was no correlation between MAPK pathway activation and overall survival (log-rank p = 0.73). The association between PPOX expression and MAPK pathway activation is biological rather than prognostic, identifying the MAPK pathway as a candidate handle for increasing 5-ALA fluorescence.

### [3] [Sensorimotor features of a reversal learning task bias decision behavior without disrupting individual difference structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08206)
*Elliot Huang,William Xu,Robert C. Wilson*

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: This study tested whether task-irrelevant sensorimotor demands bias decision-making in a probabilistic reversal learning task. While task performance didn't differ between stationary and active (walking) conditions, stay rate and win-history influence were significantly elevated in the stationary condition—but only for participants who completed the active condition first. This order-dependent effect shifted absolute measurements without disrupting individual difference structure, suggesting sensorimotor history biases cognitive measurement values but not their stability.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Computational psychiatry assumes task-irrelevant sensorimotor factors only add noise to decision behavior, yet growing evidence links sensorimotor processing to decision-making through multiple pathways. This assumption needs direct testing because if sensorimotor factors bias decision variables, they could shift patients across diagnostic thresholds in clinical applications.

Method: 90 participants completed a two-choice probabilistic reversal learning task under two sensorimotor conditions: a stationary condition (arm movements only) and an active condition (walking between physically separated computers). Task performance (propensity to choose the more probable rewarding option), stay rate (tendency to repeat previous choice), and logistic regression weights for recent win history were compared across conditions and order of completion.

Result: Task performance did not differ between conditions. Stay rate was significantly elevated in the stationary condition, but only for participants who completed the active condition first; no such difference was seen for those completing the stationary condition first. Logistic regression weights for win history showed the same order-dependent pattern. Cross-condition correlations were strong for both measures, indicating the effect shifted absolute measurements without disrupting individual difference structure.

Conclusion: Prior sensorimotor history biases the absolute values of cognitive measurements without compromising their stability across individuals. This represents a non-random source of variance that, if generalizable, could shift patients across diagnostic thresholds in clinical applications. The finding adds to the list of methodological contaminants in computational psychiatry that must be characterized and mitigated before clinical translation.

Abstract: In computational psychiatry, task-irrelevant factors such as a task's perceptual and motor features are typically assumed not to bias decision behavior, but at most to add noise. Yet growing evidence links sensorimotor processing to decision-making through multiple pathways, challenging this assumption. We tested this directly using a two-choice probabilistic reversal learning task completed by 90 participants under two sensorimotor conditions: a stationary condition requiring only arm movements to respond, and an active condition requiring participants to walk between physically separated computers. Task performance, measured as the propensity to choose the more probable rewarding option, did not differ between conditions. However, stay rate (the tendency to repeat the previous choice) was significantly elevated in the stationary condition, but only among participants who completed the active condition first; those who completed the stationary condition first showed no such difference. Logistic regression weights capturing the influence of recent win history on choice showed the same pattern, with stationary-condition elevation restricted to the active-first group. Cross-condition correlations for both measures were strong, indicating that this order-dependent effect shifted absolute measurements without disrupting individual difference structure. Prior sensorimotor history therefore does not compromise the task's ability to extract stable cognitive variables, but does bias the values it yields - a non-random source of variance that, if it generalizes beyond this experiment, could shift patients across diagnostic thresholds in proposed clinical applications. The finding adds to a growing list of potential methodological contaminants in computational psychiatry's cognitive-task paradigms that must be characterized and mitigated before clinical translation.
