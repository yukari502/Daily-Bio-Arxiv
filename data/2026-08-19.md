### [1] [The Little Scientist: LLM Agent-Driven Discovery via the Scientific Method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16951)
*Travis Smith*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: The Little Scientist is a framework where an LLM-based agent applies the scientific method iteratively—hypothesizing, implementing, testing, and receiving structured feedback—to discover novel algorithms. It achieved state-of-the-art results in protein fitness prediction (Delta V) and DNA motif discovery (DALE), outperforming existing baselines while consuming 704M tokens on CPU-only infrastructure.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The central question is whether the scientific discovery process—cycling through hypothesis, implementation, empirical testing, and feedback—can be automated by an LLM-based agent. The authors aim to move beyond simple optimization of existing components toward genuine discovery of novel algorithms.

Method: The framework consists of a "Scientist agent" that operates within an evaluation environment providing structured per-instance diagnostics. When the Scientist plateaus at a local optimum, a "Kuhn agent" injects a paradigm-shifting conjecture with cross-disciplinary inspiration to force exploration of new regions of the LLM's latent space.

Result: For protein fitness prediction, the discovered Delta V strategy ranks #1 on the ProteinGym DMS Substitutions Zero-Shot leaderboard across all five metrics. For DNA motif discovery, the DALE algorithm outperforms STREME on 132 ENCODE transcription factors (AUROC 0.842 vs 0.803) while running 11x faster. Uses 704M tokens on a single VM with no GPUs.

Conclusion: An LLM agent systematically following the scientific method can discover both new ensemble strategies and entirely new algorithms that outperform prior state-of-the-art solutions, demonstrating that LLM-based automated discovery is feasible and effective.

Abstract: What happens when you teach an LLM-based agent the scientific method?
  Motivation: Scientific discovery emerges from cycles of hypothesis, implementation, empirical testing, and feedback. Can this process be automated? We approach automated algorithm design through the lens of the scientific method, where an LLM-based agent goes through each step of the process in an ordered, iterative fashion.
  Results: We present The Little Scientist, a framework in which a "Scientist agent" works inside an evaluation environment that benchmarks its code and returns structured per-instance diagnostics. When the Scientist plateaus at a local optimum, a "Kuhn agent" injects a paradigm-shifting conjecture paired with a cross-disciplinary inspiration, forcing exploration of a different region of the LLM's latent space. We demonstrate the framework on two problems that require fundamentally different modes of discovery. For protein fitness prediction, the Scientist discovered Delta V, an ensemble calibration strategy that ranks first on the ProteinGym DMS Substitutions Zero-Shot leaderboard across all five official evaluation metrics, exceeding the #2 model (VenusREM) by +0.033 mean Spearman correlation across 217 DMS assays. This demonstrates that the framework can produce genuinely novel algorithms, not just optimize existing components. ### [2] [scDNM-VAE enables directly inspectable deep clustering of single-cell RNA-seq data through signed dendritic gating](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17228)
*Melih Agraz,Deniz Karapinar,Aysel Topsir,Qianying Cao,Erol Egrioglu,Gaurav Choudhary*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: scDNM-VAE is a deep clustering framework for single-cell RNA sequencing that combines a variational autoencoder with a dendritic neuron-inspired head, enabling directly inspectable cluster assignments governed by learnable signed synaptic weights and thresholds. It performs competitively against scVI on four benchmark datasets while offering interpretable decision functions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing deep clustering models for scRNA-seq data often rely on latent or centroid-based mechanisms that are difficult to inspect, making it hard to understand why cells are assigned to specific clusters.

Method: The framework integrates a VAE with a dendritic neuron-inspired head. Cluster assignments are controlled by learnable signed synaptic weights and thresholds, where weight sign determines gate response direction, magnitude controls steepness, and weight-threshold pairs determine transition locations, allowing direct inspection of the trained clustering function.

Result: Benchmarked on four datasets (immune, cortical, cardiac, hematopoietic cells), scDNM-VAE outperforms scVI on PBMC3k, performs comparably on Human Heart Cell Atlas and Paul15, and worse on Zeisel, while producing biologically coherent marker-gene signatures. Ablation of high-magnitude synaptic dimensions causes greater reassignment than random ablation across all datasets, though margins are modest.

Conclusion: Signed dendritic gating supports competitive clustering with a parameter-inspectable decision function, though decision-relevant information appears distributed across the latent space rather than concentrated in a few dimensions.

Abstract: Deep clustering models for single-cell RNA sequencing often assign cells through latent or centroid-based mechanisms that are difficult to inspect. We introduce scDNM-VAE (single-cell Dendritic Neuron Model Variational Autoencoder), a deep clustering framework that combines a variational autoencoder with a dendritic neuron-inspired head. Cluster assignments are governed by learnable signed synaptic weights and thresholds: the weight sign determines the direction of a gate's response to a latent coordinate, its magnitude controls steepness, and the weight-threshold pair determines the transition location. The trained clustering function can therefore be inspected directly without fitting a post-hoc explanation model.
  We benchmark scDNM-VAE on four datasets spanning immune, cortical, cardiac, and hematopoietic cells against scVI followed by KMeans and an MLP-DEC ablation. scDNM-VAE performs better than scVI on PBMC3k, comparably on the Human Heart Cell Atlas and Paul15, and worse on Zeisel, while producing biologically coherent marker-gene signatures. Ablating each cluster's three highest-magnitude synaptic dimensions causes numerically greater reassignment than random-dimension ablation across all datasets, but the margins are modest and negligible on Zeisel. These results show that signed dendritic gating supports competitive clustering with a parameter-inspectable decision function, while indicating that decision-relevant information is distributed across the latent space.

### [3] [Leveraging generative hallucination and biophysics-informed modeling for unified biomolecular sequence-structure co-design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17381)

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: MCTH (Monte Carlo Tree Hallucination) is an inference-only framework for biomolecular sequence-structure co-design that uses Monte Carlo Tree Search to optimize design trajectories over frozen pretrained folding and inverse-folding models, with optional biophysical control. It performs adaptive budget allocation across design paths using model confidence, uncertainty, and cross-expert consensus, and demonstrates improvements over simpler sampling/cycling strategies across protein-RNA, protein-DNA, protein-protein, and protein-ligand tasks, with transfer validated on held-out AlphaFold3 and Chai-1.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: De novo biomolecular interaction design remains challenging, especially for non-protein modalities like DNA/RNA which have scarce, heterogeneous complex data and stricter geometric/chemical constraints. Existing approaches lack a unified framework that can handle diverse modalities and incorporate multiple predictive models without fine-tuning.

Method: MCTH casts all-atom sequence-structure co-design as uncertainty-aware planning over hallucinated states. It treats pretrained folding and inverse-folding models as frozen black-box operators and uses Monte Carlo Tree Search to allocate a fixed inference budget across competing design trajectories. The search incorporates model confidence, uncertainty, and cross-expert consensus/disagreement, with optional integration of biophysical control within the same decision loop. No fine-tuning or backpropagation through component models is required.

Result: Matched-budget experiments across protein-RNA, protein-DNA, protein-protein, and protein-ligand design show that adaptive MCTS search outperforms simpler sampling and cycling strategies. Held-out evaluations with AlphaFold3 and Chai-1 demonstrate that the results transfer beyond the search-time oracle used during optimization.

Conclusion: MCTH provides a shared, modality-agnostic planning layer for biomolecular design that can incorporate task-specific folding, inverse-folding, and biophysical modules, achieving state-of-the-art design performance without requiring model fine-tuning or end-to-end backpropagation.

Abstract: Biomolecular design underpins applications from molecular recognition to therapeutics and synthetic biology, yet de novo interaction design remains challenging-especially for DNA/RNA, underexplored non-protein modalities with scarce, heterogeneous complex data and sharper geometric and chemical constraints. We introduce MCTH (Monte Carlo Tree Hallucination), an inference-only framework that casts all-atom sequence-structure co-design as uncertainty-aware planning over hallucinated states from pretrained folding and inverse-folding models, with optional biophysical control within the same decision loop. MCTH treats these models as frozen black-box operators and uses Monte Carlo Tree Search to allocate a fixed inference budget across competing design trajectories, incorporating model confidence and uncertainty, as well as cross-expert consensus/disagreement when multiple predictors are available. Across protein-RNA, protein-DNA, protein-protein, and protein-ligand design, matched-budget experiments show that adaptive search improves over simpler sampling and cycling strategies, while held-out AlphaFold3 and Chai-1 evaluations demonstrate transfer beyond the search-time oracle. MCTH provides a shared planning layer across modalities while allowing task-specific folding, inverse-folding, and biophysical modules, requiring no fine-tuning or backpropagation through component models.

  <summary>Details</summary>

Result: Benchmark evaluations demonstrate strong density preservation, especially on biological datasets, while retaining competitive label separability.

Conclusion: DMT-Dens provides a parametric visualization approach that balances local structure preservation with accurate density representation, improving interpretation of rare and transitional cell populations. Source code and configurations are publicly available.

Abstract: Motivation: Low-dimensional embeddings are widely used to explore cell-state heterogeneity in single-cell and other high-dimensional biological data. Results: We present DMT-Dens, a parametric manifold-visualization method built on a latent-token Transformer encoder. The model integrates rank-based manifold alignment with hard-pair aggregation. ### [5] [A Leakage-Proof Benchmark and Conformal Selective Triage for Electrohysterogram-Based Preterm Birth Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17712)
*Sunday A. Adetunji,Rhoda O. Oyewusi*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: This study establishes a patient-independent benchmark for preterm-birth prediction using electrohysterography (EHG), showing that record-grouped validation yields near-chance performance (AUROC 0.493) and demonstrates the use of class-conditional conformal selective prediction to quantify when predictions should be deferred.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Previous EHG-based preterm-birth prediction studies may have inflated performance estimates by splitting segments from the same maternal record across training and validation sets, violating patient independence. A rigorous, record-grouped benchmark is needed.

Method: All 300 records from the Term-Preterm Electrohysterogram Database (38 preterm) were analyzed. A 92-feature elastic-net logistic model was evaluated using record-grouped nested cross-validation, with preprocessing, model fitting, Platt calibration, and conformal estimation confined to training data. Performance was computed from strictly out-of-fold record-level predictions with 1,000 bootstrap resamples, and class-conditional conformal selective prediction was applied.

Result: Under patient-independent evaluation, AUROC was 0.493 (95% CI 0.467-0.520), AUPRC 0.122, and Brier score 0.115. AUROC was 0.514 at or before 26 weeks and 0.469 thereafter. At miscoverage alpha=0.10, marginal coverage was 0.897, abstention was 72.7%, and singleton-prediction accuracy was 0.624.

Conclusion: EHG features show near-chance patient-independent predictive performance for preterm birth in this dataset, establishing a record-separated reference benchmark. The key methodological principle is that the unit of resampling must correspond to the unit of intended generalization. Conformal prediction provides a principled mechanism for deferring predictions when uncertainty is high.

Abstract: Preterm birth remains a major cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality worldwide. Electrohysterography (EHG), a noninvasive measure of uterine myoelectrical activity, has been studied for preterm-birth prediction, but performance estimates may be biased when segments from the same maternal record are split across training and validation data. We formalized the distinction between segment-level and patient-independent record-grouped validation, established a patient-independent benchmark on the Term-Preterm Electrohysterogram Database, and evaluated class-conditional conformal selective prediction. At miscoverage alpha=0.10, marginal coverage was 0.897, abstention 72.7%, and singleton-prediction accuracy 0.624. These findings establish a record-separated reference benchmark for EHG prediction and a broader validation principle for segmented physiological data: the unit of resampling should correspond to the unit at which predictive performance is intended to generalize. Conformal prediction further quantifies when the available information supports a singleton classification and when uncertainty warrants deferral.

### [6] [Recovering protein conformations from single-particle cryo-EM data via indirect shape matching gradient flows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17759)
*Erik Jansson,Jonathan Krook,Ozan Öktem,Carola-Bibiane Schönlieb*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: This paper presents a novel approach for reconstructing protein backbones directly from single-particle cryo-EM projections as atomic point clouds, bypassing the conventional intermediate step of 3D electrostatic potential map reconstruction. The method formulates this as an indirect shape matching problem with a gradient flow on a Lie group, demonstrated on synthetic data for single- and multichain proteins and conformational transitions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Traditional cryo-EM reconstruction requires an intermediate step of reconstructing the 3D electrostatic potential map before building atomic models. This paper aims to bypass this bottleneck by directly reconstructing protein backbones from noisy projections, potentially improving efficiency and accuracy.

Method: The approach formulates backbone reconstruction as an indirect shape matching problem: a point-cloud template of the protein backbone is deformed until its simulated projections match the observed cryo-EM data. Since the structure is observed only through the imaging operator, the deformation is computed via a gradient flow on a Lie group, derived in a general geometric setting and then adapted for single-particle cryo-EM.

Result: On synthetic data, the method successfully recovers single-chain and multichain proteins, and can capture conformational transitions.

Conclusion: Direct backbone reconstruction from cryo-EM projections is feasible without explicit 3D map reconstruction, offering a potentially streamlined approach to structure determination.

Abstract: Single-particle cryo-electron microscopy images a macromolecule as many noisy tomographic projections of its electrostatic potential. We reconstruct the protein backbone directly from such projections, as an atomic point cloud, without the intermediate step of reconstructing the 3D electrostatic potential map. We formulate this as an indirect shape matching problem: a point-cloud template of the backbone is deformed until its simulated projections agree with the data, with the structure observed only through the imaging operator. The deformation is computed via a gradient flow on a Lie group, and we derive the resulting framework in a general geometric setting before adapting it for single-particle cryo-electron microscopy. On synthetic data, we recover single- and multichain proteins and capture conformational transitions.

Abstract: Competitive interactions can maintain diversity, yet coexistence is often fragile in well-mixed populations, where stochastic fluctuations can lead to extinction. This is the case in non-transitive systems, such as rock-paper-scissors dynamics, where no single type dominates globally.
