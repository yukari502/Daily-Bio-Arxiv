Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: The paper develops a stochastic dose-response framework integrating host infection dynamics with an explicit environmental pathogen reservoir to study invasion and persistence of environmentally transmitted pathogens. Using a continuous-time Markov chain with branching process approximation, the model incorporates direct and indirect transmission with dose-response functions, and extends to seasonal effects. Motivation: Environmentally transmitted pathogens are understudied because transmission occurs via both direct host contact and indirect environmental exposure, and infection risk depends on dose and timing. Understanding how dose-response, stochasticity, and seasonality shape pathogen invasion and persistence is a key challenge for these disease systems.

Method: A stochastic dose-response framework integrates host infection dynamics with an explicit environmental reservoir. Transmission occurs via direct host-host contact and indirect environmental exposure, with infection probability governed by dose-response functions. A stochastic continuous-time Markov chain formulation with branching process approximation estimates extinction probabilities at low host or pathogen load, extended to include seasonality in host susceptibility, environmental contact, and host-host contact.

Result: Numerical simulations on snake fungal disease show dose-response influences epidemic takeoff and infection levels. Seasonality creates windows of high and low extinction risk, with extinction probabilities strongly dependent on the route and timing of introduction within the seasonal cycle. Infection risk may depend on both pathogen dose and timing of exposure. ### [3] [Correlations at criticality in ecological communities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20086)

Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: This paper shows that ecological communities approaching marginal (critical) stability may not exhibit the strong pairwise abundance correlations previously proposed as signatures of imminent transitions. The reason is that the soft (slow) mode near criticality may not receive enough stochastic forcing to dominate fluctuations generated by other modes—spectral softness does not guarantee stochastic visibility. <details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Ecological communities are constantly reshaped by invasion, exclusion, and diversification, pushing them toward dynamical stability boundaries. Near such criticality, a soft mode is expected to dominate fluctuations and create strong abundance correlations, which have been proposed as early-warning signatures. The authors aim to test whether this expectation holds universally, as relying on such signatures could be misleading for ecological monitoring.

Method: The authors investigate whether spectral softness near marginal stability guarantees stochastic visibility of the soft mode in fluctuation patterns. They examine whether the soft mode receives enough environmental forcing to dominate the fluctuation background from all remaining modes, using three distinct ecological scenarios: a synthetic feasible community, a local community assembled via immigration from a regional pool, and a community generated through repeated diversification.

Result: In all three ecological scenarios, communities approach marginal stability without developing the near-perfect pairwise correlations predicted by the single-mode picture. This demonstrates that proximity to ecological criticality need not be visible in equal-time pairwise correlations, contradicting the previously proposed signature of imminent transitions.

Conclusion: The expectation that criticality produces dominant soft-mode fluctuations and strong pairwise correlations can fail even arbitrarily close to the stability boundary. Spectral softness is insufficient for stochastic visibility—the soft mode must also receive sufficient environmental forcing. Therefore, equal-time pairwise correlations are not a reliable indicator of proximity to ecological criticality.

Abstract: Ecological communities are continually reshaped by invasion, exclusion, and diversification, processes that naturally drive them toward the boundary of dynamical stability. Near such a boundary, a soft mode relaxes increasingly slowly and, under stochastic forcing, is expected to dominate the fluctuations, effectively reducing the dynamics to one dimension and generating strong positive and negative abundance correlations. Such correlations have therefore been proposed as signatures of an imminent transition. Here we show that this expectation can fail even arbitrarily close to criticality. The reason is that spectral softness does not guarantee stochastic visibility: the soft mode must receive enough environmental forcing to dominate the fluctuation background generated by the remaining modes. We demonstrate this mechanism in three ecological scenarios: a synthetic feasible community, a local community assembled by immigration from a regional pool, and a community generated by repeated diversification. In all three, communities approach marginal stability without developing the near-perfect pairwise correlations predicted by the single-mode picture. Thus, proximity to ecological criticality need not be visible in equal-time pairwise correlations.

### [5] [Reducing Boolean Networks via Analysis of Dynamic Network Subgraph Behavior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19292)
*Soodabeh Zakeri,Mohieddin Jafari*

Main category: q-bio.MN

TL;DR: This paper exhaustively enumerates all non-isomorphic two-node signed Boolean regulatory subgraphs with their admissible update rules, and characterizes their state-transition graphs under both synchronous and asynchronous updating. The analysis reveals that despite structural diversity, many graph-rule combinations converge to a limited number of dynamical classes, highlighting redundancy between structure and dynamics. A web application (BORNA) is provided for exploration and reproducibility.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Boolean networks are compact models for regulatory systems, but their rapidly expanding state spaces make systematic dynamical analysis challenging. The authors aim to move beyond static wiring diagrams toward a behavior-based classification of minimal network structures, and to compare how different update schemes (synchronous vs. asynchronous) affect dynamical outcomes.

Method: The authors systematically enumerate all non-isomorphic two-node signed regulatory subgraphs with admissible Boolean update rules, then exhaustively characterize their state-transition graphs (STGs) under both synchronous and asynchronous updating. They extract quantitative dynamical descriptors for behavior-based classification, comparing deterministic fixed-point/cyclic attractor regimes (synchronous) against terminal strongly connected components of asynchronous STGs. They also develop BORNA, an interactive web application for exploring the catalogue and simulations.

Result: Despite diverse graph-rule combinations, many realizations converge to a limited repertoire of dynamical classes, revealing substantial redundancy between structural and dynamical representations. The comparison of update schemes distinguishes behaviors robust across update assumptions from those dependent on synchronous updating. A testable framework is proposed for using local dynamical classes to characterize subnetworks within larger Boolean models.

Conclusion: The results provide a systematic reference for minimal Boolean network dynamics and serve as a foundation for extending behavior-based analysis to larger regulatory systems. The BORNA web application facilitates exploration and reproducibility of the complete network catalogue, STGs, and simulations.

Abstract: Boolean networks provide a compact framework for modeling regulatory systems, yet their rapidly expanding state spaces make systematic dynamical analysis challenging. Here, we systematically enumerate all non-isomorphic two-node signed regulatory subgraphs with their admissible Boolean update rules and exhaustively characterize their state-transition graphs (STGs) under both synchronous and asynchronous updating. By extracting quantitative dynamical descriptors, we move beyond static wiring diagrams toward a behavior-based classification of network structures. The synchronous analysis identifies deterministic fixed-point and cyclic attractor regimes, while the asynchronous analysis captures one-node-at-a-time dynamics through terminal strongly connected components of the asynchronous STGs. Comparing the two update schemes distinguishes dynamical behaviors that are robust across update assumptions from those that depend on synchronous updating. Despite the diversity of graph-rule combinations, many realizations converge to a limited repertoire of dynamical classes, revealing substantial redundancy between structural and dynamical representations. We further propose a testable framework for investigating whether such local dynamical classes can be used to characterize selected subnetworks within larger Boolean models. To facilitate exploration and reproducibility, we developed BORNA, an interactive web application for exploring the complete network catalogue, STGs, and synchronous and asynchronous simulations, available at https://jafarilab.github.io/BORNA/. Together, these results provide a systematic reference for minimal Boolean network dynamics and a foundation for extending behavior-based analysis to larger regulatory systems.
