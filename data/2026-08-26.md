### [1] [InfoDPP-PAC: Principled Patch Selection for Whole Slide Image Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23574)
*Prateek Mittal,Ayush Srivastava,Joohi Chauhan*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: InfoDPP-PAC is a principled patch-selection framework for whole-slide images that combines Gaussian process relevance modelling, determinantal diversity, submodular greedy optimisation, and an adaptive stopping rule. It reduces patch usage by 83.7% while retaining 97.9% of full-budget selection quality on 202 gastrointestinal WSIs, and achieves the highest teacher-derived relevance score among fourteen baselines at a matched budget.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing bag-construction strategies for WSI analysis either fail to control redundancy (Uniform extraction, handcrafted heuristics), couple patch importance to a specific downstream classifier (attention-based MIL), or optimise only embedding-space coverage without task-relevant quality (coreset methods). A principled selection framework that simultaneously models patch quality, diversity, and cardinality is needed.

Method: InfoDPP-PAC combines four components: (1) teacher-seeded Gaussian process relevance modelling for patch quality, (2) determinantal log-determinant diversity for non-redundancy, (3) submodular greedy optimisation, and (4) a concentration-based adaptive stopping rule. Theoretically, it proves the log-determinant diversity term equals the GP mutual information between the selected subset and the latent relevance function, and derives a PAC-style certificate for residual information gain allowing slide-varying cardinality.

Result: On 202 HISTAI gastrointestinal WSIs, the adaptive rule uses 83.7% fewer patches on average than a fixed full budget while retaining 97.9% of full-budget composite selection quality. At a matched budget, InfoDPP-PAC achieves the highest mean teacher-derived relevance score among fourteen baselines, with diversity and composite scores close to the strongest coreset methods.

Conclusion: InfoDPP-PAC is positioned as a controlled quality-diversity-cardinality patch selection framework rather than a downstream clinical predictor. It does not claim end-to-end diagnostic improvement after retraining a downstream MIL model, but demonstrates principled selection with strong quality, diversity, and efficiency properties.

Abstract: Each WSI slide contains thousands of candidate tissue patches, while supervision is usually available only at slide level. Existing bag-construction strategies like Uniform extraction and handcrafted heuristics do not control redundancy while attention-based multiple-instance models couple patch importance to a particular downstream classifier, and coreset methods optimise embedding-space coverage without modelling task-relevant patch quality. We introduce InfoDPP-PAC, a principled patch-selection framework that combines teacher-seeded Gaussian process relevance modelling, determinantal log-determinant diversity,submodular greedy optimisation, and a concentration-based adaptive stopping rule. The main theoretical result shows that the log-determinant diversity term used in DPP-style selection is the Gaussian process mutual information between a selected subset and the latent relevance function. We further derive a PAC-style certificate for residual information gain, allowing the number of retained patches to vary by slide rather than being fixed a priori. The empirical study evaluates whether the selected subset is diverse, spatially and morphologically covering, non-redundant, and enriched for the teacher-derived relevance signal. It does not claim end-to-end diagnostic improvement after retraining a downstream MIL model. On 202 HISTAI gastrointestinal whole-slide images, the adaptive rule uses 83.7% fewer patches on average than a fixed full budget while retaining 97.9% of full-budget composite selection quality. At a matched budget, InfoDPP-PAC achieves the highest mean teacher-derived relevance score among fourteen baselines, with diversity and composite scores close to the strongest coreset methods. The results support InfoDPP-PAC as a controlled quality-diversity-cardinality selection framework, rather than as a downstream clinical predictor.

### [2] [A nonlinear dynamics approach to assess amplitude-frequency coupling in non-invasive human electrogastrography recordings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23613)
*Akshita A. Rao,Todd P. Coleman*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: The paper develops a statistical nonlinear-dynamics framework that models gastric-band EGG signals as a stochastic Stuart-Landau oscillator to estimate local effective phase-shear (β_eff), a normalized measure of amplitude-frequency coupling. Applied to healthy participants and those with gastric dysfunction, the measure is robust, reproducible, differs between groups, and classifies dysfunction comparably to multielectrode phase-based traveling-wave measures.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional EGG analyses rely on dominant frequency, power, or phase-based metrics that do not explicitly model amplitude-dependent frequency dynamics. The authors aim to establish amplitude-frequency coupling as a complementary dynamical feature of gastric slow-wave activity.

Method: The authors model the gastric-band analytic EGG signal as a stochastic Stuart-Landau oscillator and estimate a normalized measure of amplitude-frequency coupling called local effective phase-shear (β_eff). They validate it with held-out data and amplitude surrogates, then apply it to fasted/fed recordings from healthy participants and those with gastric dysfunction.

Result: Effective phase-shear was consistently negative in 60 healthy participants, generalized to held-out halves of recordings, and was abolished by amplitude surrogates. Phase-shear magnitude differed between healthy and dysfunctional groups and classified gastric dysfunction comparably to the strongest multielectrode phase-based traveling-wave measures.

Conclusion: Amplitude-frequency coupling is a genuine dynamical feature of gastric-band EGG that complements conventional spectral and spatial phase metrics. Future work should develop multichannel observation models to determine its relationship to slow-wave propagation and gastric function.

Abstract: Conventional electrogastrography (EGG) analyses summarize gastric slow-wave activity using dominant frequency, power, or phase-based metrics, without explicitly modeling amplitude-dependent frequency dynamics. We developed a statistical nonlinear-dynamics framework that represents the gastric-band analytic EGG signal as a stochastic Stuart-Landau oscillator and estimate local effective phase-shear, $β_{\mathrm{eff}}$, a normalized measure of amplitude-frequency coupling. In overnight recordings from 60 healthy participants, effective phase-shear was consistently negative, generalized to held-out halves of each recording, and was abolished by amplitude surrogates that disrupted amplitude-phase alignment while preserving amplitude-series structure. We then applied the measure to fasted and fed recordings from 16 healthy participants and 24 participants with gastric dysfunction. Phase-shear magnitude differed between groups and classified dysfunction comparable to the strongest multielectrode phase-based traveling-wave measures. These findings establish amplitude-frequency coupling as a dynamical feature of gastric-band EGG that complements conventional spectral and spatial phase metrics, while motivating future multichannel observation models to determine its relationship to slow-wave propagation and gastric function.

### [5] [Optimizing RNA yield using deep neural networks coupled to massively parallel screening](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23722)
*Dinghai Zheng,Justin Hong,Jun Wang,Adrien Villain,Mickaël Costallat,Fernando Ulloa Montoya,Vikram Agarwal*

Main category: q-bio.GN

TL;DR: This paper presents an integrated deep learning framework that uses massively parallel NGS assays to measure and predict RNA yield from in vitro transcription (IVT) across large sequence spaces, achieving a Pearson correlation of 0.94 on held-out test data and enabling pre-experimental prioritization of manufacturable RNA sequence designs.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: mRNA therapeutics are powerful but face a critical bottleneck in manufacturing RNA economically. The influence of promoter-adjacent DNA sequences on RNA yield is poorly characterized, creating a need for scalable, data-driven approaches to predict and optimize RNA manufacturability.

Method: A library of 10^5 randomized oligonucleotide sequences was designed to explore sequence diversity within a defined structural context. DNA and RNA abundances were quantified in parallel via Illumina sequencing. Sequences were one-hot encoded and used to train a convolutional neural network (CNN) to predict RNA yield from sequence.

Result: The CNN model achieved a Pearson correlation of 0.94 between predicted and experimentally measured RNA yield on a held-out test set, demonstrating strong generalization across diverse sequence contexts. The model can be deployed in production to score and rank novel RNA sequences by predicted IVT yield.

Conclusion: This framework establishes a scalable, data-driven approach to DNA and RNA sequence optimization with broad applicability to vaccine antigen design, therapeutic protein delivery, and synthetic biology, significantly reducing screening costs and accelerating RNA engineering cycle times.

Abstract: Messenger RNA (mRNA)-based therapeutics have emerged as a powerful platform for vaccines, protein replacement therapies, and cancer immunotherapy. A critical bottleneck in mRNA development is manufacturing large quantities of RNA economically, as measured by RNA yield emerging from an in vitro transcription (IVT) reaction. However, how promoter-adjacent DNA sequences influence RNA yield remains poorly characterized. Here, we present an integrated deep learning framework that leverages massively parallel next-generation sequencing (NGS) assays to measure RNA yield across large sequence spaces. A library of 10^5 randomized oligonucleotide sequences was designed to systematically explore sequence diversity within a defined structural context. DNA and RNA abundances were quantified in parallel using Illumina sequencing, enabling high-resolution measurement of sequence-to-yield relationships at scale. Importantly, the trained model can be deployed in a production environment to score and rank novel RNA sequence designs by predicted IVT yield, enabling cost-effective, pre-experimental prioritization of the most manufacturable candidates. This framework establishes a scalable, data-driven approach to DNA and RNA sequence optimization, with broad applicability to vaccine antigen design, therapeutic protein delivery, and synthetic biology. By integrating high-throughput experimentation with advanced deep learning modeling, it significantly reduces screening costs and accelerates RNA engineering cycle times.

### [6] [Global geometry of the genotype-phenotype map illuminates a trade-off between penetrance and mutational adaptability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24704)
*Yutaro Ikeda,Kunihiko Kaneko,Tetsuhiro S. Hatakeyama*

Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: This paper uses an abstract statistical physics-inspired model of genotype-phenotype mapping to exhaustively reconstruct fitness landscapes, revealing a global geometric trade-off between penetrance (reliable expression of the current phenotype) and mutational adaptability (access to alternative high-fitness phenotypes). High-penetrance genotypes occupy fitness-region interiors and are mutationally robust, while low-penetrance genotypes lie near boundaries with greater mutational access. Evolutionary simulations show environmental conditions determine the balance: stable environments favor penetrance, while frequent environmental change favors adaptability.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Previous studies suggested high penetrance may restrict mutational access to alternative phenotypes, but because they focused only on evolved genotypes and local mutational neighborhoods, they could not determine whether this local constraint limits mutational adaptability under environmental change. Addressing this requires reconstructing the full probability distribution over phenotypes for every genotype and environment-specific fitness landscapes, which is generally infeasible due to combinatorial growth of genotype and phenotype spaces.

Method: The authors develop an abstract model of stochastic genotype-phenotype mapping inspired by interacting spins in statistical physics, which permits exhaustive reconstruction of the full map. They analyze the global geometry of high-fitness regions across genotype space, then run evolutionary simulations under different environmental conditions to test how phenotypic noise and environmental change rate affect the evolvability-penetrance balance.

Result: High-penetrance genotypes tend to occupy the interior of environment-specific high-fitness regions and are mutationally robust, whereas lower-penetrance genotypes tend to lie near boundaries with greater mutational access to high-fitness regions for alternative environments. Stronger phenotypic noise in a fixed environment favors high penetrance and mutational robustness, while frequent environmental change favors mutational accessibility at the expense of penetrance.

Conclusion: Penetrance and mutational adaptability are opposing consequences of the same global geometry of fitness landscapes, with environmental conditions determining their evolutionary balance. This provides a unified framework connecting phenotypic reliability, mutational robustness, and evolvability.

Abstract: Evolution in changing environments requires both reliable expression of the currently favored phenotype, as quantified by penetrance, and the capacity to reach alternative phenotypes through mutation. Previous studies suggest that high penetrance may restrict such mutational access. However, because these studies focus on evolved genotypes and local mutational neighborhoods, they cannot determine whether this local constraint limits mutational adaptability under environmental change. Such adaptability depends on a genotype's position relative to high-fitness regions for other environments. Addressing this question requires reconstructing the full probability distribution over phenotypes for every genotype and the resulting environment-specific fitness landscapes across genotype space. Such reconstruction is generally infeasible because genotype and phenotype spaces grow combinatorially. Here, an abstract model of stochastic genotype-phenotype mapping, inspired by interacting spins in statistical physics, permits exhaustive reconstruction of the map. We find that high-penetrance genotypes tend to occupy the interior of environment-specific high-fitness regions and are mutationally robust, whereas lower-penetrance genotypes tend to lie near their boundaries and have greater mutational access to high-fitness regions for alternative environments. This global geometry generates a trade-off between penetrance and mutational adaptability. In evolutionary simulations, stronger phenotypic noise in a fixed environment increases the selective advantage of reliable expression, thereby favoring high penetrance and mutational robustness. Frequent environmental change instead favors mutational accessibility at the expense of penetrance. Thus, penetrance and adaptability are opposing consequences of the same global geometry, with environmental conditions determining their evolutionary balance.

</details>
