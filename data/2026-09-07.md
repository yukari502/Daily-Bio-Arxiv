### [1] [MarkerScout: A Disease-Agnostic Machine Learning Framework for Biomarker Prediction from Multi-Scale Mechanistic Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04268)
*Robert Moore,Frank Agayie-Ntim,Lindsey B. Crawford,M. Jana Broadhurst,David M. Brett-Major,Prakash Packrisamy,Ahmed Abdeen Hamed,Tomas Helikar*

Main category: q-bio.OT

TL;DR: MarkerScout is a biomarker discovery framework demonstrated on three infectious diseases (SARS-CoV-2, Influenza A Virus, Plasmodium falciparum) across hospitalization and ICU cohorts. It achieves cross-validated macro F1 scores from 0.82 to 0.99 and produces tiered, direction-aware biomarker lists. When benchmarked against independent clinical ICU datasets, its top-ranked features outperform 94.4% of random feature sets for SARS-CoV-2, with weaker but directionally consistent advantages for IAV (66.7%) and Pf (60.7%). IL-18 emerged as a strong, consistently directed biomarker across SARS-CoV-2 phases.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The paper aims to validate a biomarker discovery framework (MarkerScout) across multiple infectious diseases and clinical cohorts, demonstrating that machine-learning-driven feature selection can produce robust, direction-aware, and generalizable biomarkers validated against independent datasets.

Method: MarkerScout is applied to six cohorts derived from a companion mechanistic immune-simulation platform, covering three diseases (SARS-CoV-2, IAV, Plasmodium falciparum) across hospitalization and ICU settings. The framework produces tiered, direction-aware biomarker lists per disease/phase, and top-ranked features are benchmarked against three independently collected clinical ICU datasets using percentile-based comparison against random feature sets.

Result: Best-pipeline cross-validated macro F1 ranged from 0.82 (IAV-HOSP) to 0.99 (COV-ICU). IL-18 reached the strongest tier in both SARS-CoV-2 phases with consistent direction. Top-ranked features outperformed 94.4% of random feature sets for SARS-CoV-2 ICU, 66.7% for IAV, and 60.7% for Plasmodium falciparum in independent clinical benchmarks.

Conclusion: MarkerScout demonstrates strong performance and external validity for SARS-CoV-2 biomarker discovery, with directionally consistent but weaker generalization to Influenza A Virus and Plasmodium falciparum. IL-18 is identified as a robust, reproducible biomarker for SARS-CoV-2 across disease phases.

Abstract: We demonstrate the framework on three infectious diseases derived from a companion mechanistic immune-simulation platform: SARS-CoV-2, Influenza A Virus, and Plasmodium falciparum. Each disease was evaluated across hospitalization and intensive care unit cohorts, yielding six cohorts in total. Best-pipeline cross-validated macro F1 ranged from 0.82 for IAV-HOSP to 0.99 for COV-ICU, and the framework produced tiered, direction-aware biomarker lists for each disease and phase. Interleukin-18 (IL-18) reached the strongest tier in both SARS-CoV-2 phases with consistent direction.
  When benchmarked against three separate, independently collected clinical ICU datasets, MarkerScout's top-ranked features outperformed 94.4% of randomly selected feature sets of equivalent size for SARS-CoV-2, with a weaker but directionally consistent advantage for Influenza A Virus (66.7%) and Plasmodium falciparum (60.7%).

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: This perspective paper provides a foundational overview of MEG foundation models, covering key design choices (tokenization, sensor/source space, geometry encoding, architectures, objectives, data) and offering a development roadmap spanning native MEG pretraining, EEG foundation model adaptation, time-series transfer, and multi-modal integration, while emphasizing the need for coordinated infrastructure and responsible data practices.

The authors aim to establish basic concepts and clarify design choices to accelerate progress in this emerging field.

Method: The paper takes a perspective/high-level review approach: it systematically lays out the conceptual foundations of MEG foundation models, provides a didactic overview of design choices (tokenization, sensor- vs source-space, sensor-geometry encoding, backbone architectures, self-supervised objectives, pretraining data), and proposes a future roadmap organized around four development paths (native MEG pretraining, EEG FM adaptation, generic time-series transfer, and multi-modal integration with EEG, fMRI, MRI, behavior, and stimuli).

Result: The paper identifies the current limitations of MEG foundation models, systematically categorizes their key design decisions, and outlines concrete future directions and infrastructure requirements, including the need for diverse reusable datasets, rigorous cross-subject/site/task/clinical evaluation, and responsible data governance.

Abstract: Foundation models are beginning to reshape brain-signal analysis by moving the field beyond task-specific decoding pipelines toward reusable models pretrained on broad neural datasets. Magnetoencephalography (MEG) is a compelling but still underdeveloped target for this shift: it captures human cortical dynamics at millisecond resolution while offering stronger spatial interpretability than EEG, making it especially valuable for source-resolved studies of perception, language, cognition, and clinical brain function. Yet MEG foundation models remain at an early stage, with only a small number of MEG-specific and MEG-inclusive multi-modal models, modest pretraining corpora, and emerging but still limited benchmarks. We then offer a roadmap for future development, organized around native MEG pretraining, adaptation of EEG foundation models, transfer from generic time-series models, and multi-modal integration with EEG, fMRI, MRI, behaviour, and stimulus features. We highlight the need for coordinated infrastructure, including diverse and reusable MEG datasets, rigorous evaluation across subjects, sites, tasks, and clinical settings, and responsible data-sharing practices that address consent, privacy, access, and governance.

Main category: q-bio.BM

Four ligand pairs demonstrated significantly better binding affinities than current phase-III inhibitors, suggesting promising avenues for combination therapies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>

Therefore, there is a pressing need to discover more effective therapeutic approaches for this progressive neurodegenerative disorder.

Method: The study employed multi-ligand simultaneous docking (MLSD) to screen a library of small molecules with known IC50 values against BACE1. Compounds were filtered to exclude Pan-Assay Interference (PAINS) and "Brenk" flagged compounds. Single-ligand docking was first performed to establish individual ligand-protein interactions, which formed the basis for creating ligand pairs subsequently tested via MLSD. Results were compared against four phase-III BACE1 inhibitors as standards.

Conclusion: MLSD holds transformative potential for identifying synergistic compound interactions that outperform current single-agent BACE1 inhibitors. The identified ligand pairs pave the way for novel combination therapies in Alzheimer's disease treatment.

The recent phase III drugs such as Atabecestat, Elenbecestat, Lanabecestat, and Verubecestat were considered to be standards for comparison in this study. A library of small molecules with known IC50 values against BACE1 was filtered for flagging Pan-Assay Interference (PAINS) and "Brenk" compounds. ### [4] [The physical foundation beneath protein generative modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04465)
*Tianyu Lu,Po-Ssu Huang*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: This review examines the role of physics in protein generative modeling, arguing that while diffusion-based generative models have replaced physical equations for de novo protein structure sampling, they can learn non-physical features and fail in out-of-distribution settings. The authors advocate for physically grounded learned representations and integrating experimental data into generative models as foundational for functional protein design.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Physical equations in protein modeling have been largely replaced by generative models trained on structure data. While these models are more efficient for de novo sampling, they can learn non-physical features and break down in out-of-distribution settings typical of protein design campaigns. This motivates a review of where physics persists in these pipelines and what issues arise when physical components are unmodeled.

Method: This is a perspective/review paper that surveys protein generative modeling pipelines, highlighting where physics remains embedded in the workflow, and identifying problems caused by neglecting physically relevant components of macromolecular systems. It presents a viewpoint advocating for physically grounded learned representations and updating generative models with experimental data.

Result: The review identifies that while generative models have made de novo protein structure sampling highly efficient, they risk learning non-physical features and failing in out-of-distribution contexts. It highlights lingering issues when physical components of macromolecular systems are left unmodeled and argues that physically grounded representations combined with experimental data integration are foundational for functional protein design.

Conclusion: Respecting underlying physics through physically grounded learned representations and incorporating experimental data into generative models is essential for achieving robust, functional protein design, especially in out-of-distribution scenarios.

Abstract: Physical equations in protein modeling appear to have been replaced by generative models trained directly on structure data. By learning a mapping from noise to data, sampling de novo protein structures has become much more efficient. However, such models can also learn non-physical features and break down with out-of-distribution settings which are typical in protein design campaigns. In this review, we highlight where the physics persists in protein generative modeling pipelines and the issues that linger when physically relevant components of a macromolecular system are left unmodeled. We present a perspective that respecting the underlying physics of macromolecular systems, increasingly through learned representations that are physically grounded and updating generative models with experimental data, is foundational to generative modeling for functional protein design.

*Michał Kulczykowski,Rafał Łabędzki*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: Pretraining molecular graphs with LeJEPA/SIGReg improves learned representations but does not robustly improve finetuning. Pretrained embeddings outperform random initialisation on frozen probes and, when truncated and combined with Morgan fingerprints, boost ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC from 0.805 to 0.832. However, finetuning gains are weak, partition-dependent, and null across most splits and encoder settings.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The value of self-supervised pretraining for molecular graph neural networks is contested despite its success in language and vision, motivating a rigorous evaluation of whether pretraining on large unlabelled molecular corpora actually improves downstream molecular property prediction.

Method: Adapts LeJEPA (a predictor-free joint-embedding predictive architecture with SIGReg regularisation) to molecular graphs. Evaluates GPS and Chemprop-style D-MPNN encoders on antibiotic-activity (Wong et al.) and ogbg-molhiv datasets using multi-seed bootstrap-based protocols, frozen probes, finetuning, dimensionality truncation, and feature-level combination with Morgan fingerprints.

Result: Frozen probe on pretrained embeddings beats random initialisation on both tasks (ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC 0.788 vs 0.665). Finetuning gains are null except one scaffold split (delta AUPRC +0.041, p=0.010) that vanishes across five partitions (pooled +0.013, p=0.095). Truncated embeddings combined with Morgan fingerprints raise ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC from 0.805 to 0.832 (delta +0.027; p=0.014).

Conclusion: Pretraining supplies complementary molecular information best exploited via feature-level representation combination rather than finetuning. Finetuning benefits are weak and partition-dependent, while frozen embeddings combined with classical fingerprints provide robust gains.

Abstract: Self-supervised pretraining has transformed language and vision, but its value for molecular graph neural networks remains contested. We ask whether pretraining on a large unlabelled corpus improves molecular property prediction. We adapt LeJEPA, a predictor-free joint-embedding predictive architecture regularised by Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularisation (SIGReg), to molecular graphs, evaluating GPS and Chemprop-style D-MPNN encoders on the Wong et al. [1] antibiotic-activity dataset and ogbg-molhiv using a multi-seed, bootstrap-based protocol. Pretraining improves learned representations but does not robustly improve finetuning. A frozen probe on pretrained embeddings exceeds random initialisation on both tasks (ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC 0.788 vs 0.665; +0.123), reaching the published self-supervised band, but this does not translate into finetuning gains. On the antibiotic scaffold split, a canonical partition is significant (delta AUPRC +0.041, p = 0.010), but the effect vanishes across five partitions (pooled +0.013, p = 0.095). Finetuning is null on the random split, ogbg-molhiv, and D-MPNN. The representational edge is nevertheless recoverable. Embeddings saturate at ~16-32 effective dimensions, whereas Morgan fingerprints improve to 1024 bits. At matched dimensionality, fingerprints lead validation (0.799 vs 0.782 at 128 dimensions) but trail shifted test scaffolds (0.759 vs 0.788). Truncating embeddings and combining them with a 1024-bit Morgan fingerprint raises ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC from 0.805 to 0.832 (delta +0.027; 95% CI [+0.003, +0.054]; p = 0.014); an untrained encoder gains nothing (delta -0.003). Thus, pretraining supplies complementary information best realised through feature-level combination, while finetuning gains are weak and partition-dependent.

### [6] [Towards AI-Driven Nanomedicine Discovery: A Benchmark and Multimodal Learning Framework for Nano Self-Assembly Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04278)

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: This paper introduces NSA-Bench, the first public benchmark for nano self-assembly (NSA) prediction, formalized as a binary classification task between molecular pairs. The authors develop NSA-Net, an interaction-aware multimodal framework integrating graph topology, sequence semantics, and physicochemical descriptors, achieving ROC-AUC of 0.9470±0.0112 (Small) and 0.9492±0.0062 (Large), surpassing baselines by 3.9–17.1 percentage points.

Motivation: Nano self-assembly has great potential for nanomedicine discovery, but current discovery relies on costly wet-lab screening. Existing machine learning approaches lack standardized tasks, effective pairwise compatibility modeling, and public benchmarks with unified evaluation.

Method: Formalize NSA prediction as binary classification between molecular pairs; establish NSA-Bench (first public benchmark with curated molecular combinations, conditions, labels, and evaluation protocols); develop NSA-Net, a multimodal framework integrating graph topology, sequence semantics, and physicochemical descriptors for molecular-pair representation learning. Abstract: Nano self-assembly organizes molecular components into bioactive nanoscale structures.
