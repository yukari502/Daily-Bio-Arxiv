### [1] [Self-Supervised Pretraining of Molecular Graph Encoders with LeJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04261)
*Michał Kulczykowski,Rafał Łabędzki*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: This paper investigates whether self-supervised pretraining on molecular graphs improves downstream property prediction. Using LeJEPA with SIGReg regularisation on GPS and D-MPNN encoders, the authors find pretraining improves frozen-probe representations (ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC 0.788 vs 0.665 for random init) but does not robustly improve finetuning across most splits and models. The representational value is best harvested via feature-level combination: truncating pretrained embeddings and concatenating with 1024-bit Morgan fingerprints raises ROC-AUC from 0.805 to 0.832, while an untrained encoder gains nothing.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Self-supervised pretraining has transformed language and vision, but its utility for molecular graph neural networks is unresolved; the authors aim to rigorously test whether pretraining on unlabelled molecular corpora translates into improved molecular property prediction.

Method: Adapts LeJEPA (a predictor-free joint-embedding predictive architecture regularised by Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularisation / SIGReg) to molecular graphs, using GPS and Chemprop-style D-MPNN encoders. Evaluates on the Wong et al. antibiotic-activity dataset and ogbg-molhiv with a multi-seed, bootstrap-based protocol, probing and finetuning, and comparing truncated embeddings combined with Morgan fingerprints.

Result: Frozen probes on pretrained embeddings beat random initialisation on both tasks (ogbg-molhiv +0.123), but finetuning gains are weak and largely non-significant—significant on one canonical antibiotic scaffold split (+0.041 AUPRC, p=0.010) yet vanishing across five partitions (p=0.095), and null on random splits, ogbg-molhiv, and D-MPNN. Embeddings saturate at ~16-32 dimensions vs 1024-bit fingerprints; at matched dimensions fingerprints lead validation but trail shifted scaffolds. Combining truncated embeddings with Morgan fingerprints improves ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC from 0.805 to 0.832 (p=0.014); untrained encoders gain nothing.

Conclusion: Pretraining on molecular graphs provides complementary information best exploited through feature-level combination rather than finetuning, whose gains are weak and partition-dependent. The authors recommend re-evaluating how pretrained molecular representations are integrated into downstream tasks.

Abstract: Self-supervised pretraining has transformed language and vision, but its value for molecular graph neural networks remains contested. We ask whether pretraining on a large unlabelled corpus improves molecular property prediction. We adapt LeJEPA, a predictor-free joint-embedding predictive architecture regularised by Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularisation (SIGReg), to molecular graphs, evaluating GPS and Chemprop-style D-MPNN encoders on the Wong et al. [1] antibiotic-activity dataset and ogbg-molhiv using a multi-seed, bootstrap-based protocol. Pretraining improves learned representations but does not robustly improve finetuning. A frozen probe on pretrained embeddings exceeds random initialisation on both tasks (ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC 0.788 vs 0.665; +0.123), reaching the published self-supervised band, but this does not translate into finetuning gains. On the antibiotic scaffold split, a canonical partition is significant (delta AUPRC +0.041, p = 0.010), but the effect vanishes across five partitions (pooled +0.013, p = 0.095). Finetuning is null on the random split, ogbg-molhiv, and D-MPNN. The representational edge is nevertheless recoverable. Embeddings saturate at ~16-32 effective dimensions, whereas Morgan fingerprints improve to 1024 bits. At matched dimensionality, fingerprints lead validation (0.799 vs 0.782 at 128 dimensions) but trail shifted test scaffolds (0.759 vs 0.788). Truncating embeddings and combining them with a 1024-bit Morgan fingerprint raises ogbg-molhiv ROC-AUC from 0.805 to 0.832 (delta +0.027; 95% CI [+0.003, +0.054]; p = 0.014); an untrained encoder gains nothing (delta -0.003). Thus, pretraining supplies complementary information best realised through feature-level combination, while finetuning gains are weak and partition-dependent.

### [2] [Towards AI-Driven Nanomedicine Discovery: A Benchmark and Multimodal Learning Framework for Nano Self-Assembly Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04278)
*Quan Hao,Mengyue Fan,Zifan Dong,Jianduo Zhao,Changhao Xiao,Shangqing Jiao,Hao Zhang,Yudong Wang,Fei Xia,Jigang Wang,Liguo Zhang,Chong Qiu*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: This paper introduces NSA-Bench, the first public benchmark for nano self-assembly (NSA) prediction between molecular pairs, and NSA-Net, an interaction-aware multimodal framework integrating graph topology, sequence semantics, and physicochemical descriptors. NSA-Net achieves ROC-AUC of 0.9470±0.0112 (Small) and 0.9492±0.0062 (Large), outperforming machine-learning and graph-based baselines by 3.9 and 17.1 percentage points respectively on the Small track.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Nano self-assembly has great potential for nanomedicine discovery (e.g., anti-lung-cancer therapies from Chinese herbal formulas), but discovery relies on costly wet-lab screening. Existing machine learning approaches lack standardized tasks, effective pairwise compatibility modeling, and public benchmarks with unified evaluation.

Method: The authors formalize NSA prediction as a binary classification task predicting self-assembly between molecular pairs, establish NSA-Bench with curated molecular combinations, experimental conditions, labels, and standardized evaluation protocols, and develop NSA-Net—an interaction-aware multimodal framework that fuses graph topology, sequence semantics, and physicochemical descriptors to learn molecular-pair representations.

Result: NSA-Net achieves ROC-AUC of 0.9470±0.0112 on Small and 0.9492±0.0062 on Large tracks, surpassing the strongest ML baselines by 3.9 percentage points and graph-based baselines by 17.1 percentage points on the Small track. Representation analyses reveal interpretable molecular characteristics, and an NSA-Agent case study demonstrates experimental-condition-aware reasoning for formulation refinement.

Conclusion: The proposed benchmark and model advance nano self-assembly prediction by providing standardized evaluation and effective pairwise compatibility modeling, with potential to reduce reliance on costly wet-lab screening and support formulation optimization in nanomedicine discovery.

Abstract: Nano self-assembly organizes molecular components into bioactive nanoscale structures. Self-assembled nanoparticles (NAPs) derived from Chinese herbal formulas and applications such as anti-lung-cancer therapy demonstrate the substantial potential of self-assembly for nanomedicine discovery. Yet discovery still relies on costly wet-lab screening, while existing machine learning approaches lack standardized tasks, effective pairwise compatibility modeling, and public benchmarks with unified evaluation. To address these limitations, we formalize NSA prediction as a binary classification task for predicting self-assembly between molecular pairs and then establish NSA-Bench, the first public benchmark with curated molecular combinations, experimental conditions, self-assembly labels, and standardized evaluation protocols. We further develop NSA-Net, an interaction-aware multimodal framework that integrates complementary molecular evidence from graph topology, sequence semantics, and physicochemical descriptors to learn molecular-pair representations for self-assembly prediction. Extensive experiments on NSA-Bench show that NSA-Net achieves a ROC-AUC of $0.9470\pm0.0112$ (Small) and $0.9492\pm0.0062$ (Large). On the Small track, it surpasses the strongest machine-learning and graph-based baselines by 3.9 and 17.1 percentage points, respectively. Representation analyses reveal interpretable molecular characteristics associated with self-assembly prediction captured by the learned representations. Moreover, an NSA-Agent case study further demonstrates how NSA-Net predictions can support formulation refinement through experimental-condition-aware reasoning. Our code is available at https://github.com/developer-hq/NSA-Net.

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: This study applies multi-ligand simultaneous docking (MLSD) to identify synergistic inhibitor combinations for BACE1, an enzyme central to Alzheimer's disease pathology. Screening thousands of small molecules against phase-III inhibitors (Atabecestat, Elenbecestat, Lanabecestat, Verubecestat), the authors identified four ligand pairs with superior binding affinities and inter-ligand interactions, suggesting synergistic effects that could outperform current single-drug therapies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder with no curative therapies. While BACE1 inhibitors (targeting amyloid plaque formation) have advanced to phase-III trials, they suffer from side effects and insufficient efficacy in slowing cognitive decline. The authors aim to discover synergistic multi-drug combinations that outperform existing single phase-III inhibitors.

Method: The study employs multi-ligand simultaneous docking (MLSD), a computational technique allowing multiple ligands to dock simultaneously. A small-molecule library with known IC50 values for BACE1 was filtered to remove PAINS and Brenk flagged compounds. Single-ligand docking established individual ligand interactions, which informed the construction of ligand pairs subsequently tested via MLSD. Binding affinities were compared against four phase-III BACE1 inhibitors as standards.

Result: Four ligand pairs demonstrated superior binding affinities relative to phase-III inhibitors: CHEMBL4078427 + CHEMBL3656158 (-19.90 kcal/mol), CHEMBL4078427 + CHEMBL3695732 (-18.45 kcal/mol), Verubecestat + CHEMBL3656158 (-18.07 kcal/mol), and CHEMBL4078427 + Lanabecestat (-17.67 kcal/mol). Inter-ligand interactions indicated synergistic effects within these combinations.

Conclusion: MLSD shows transformative potential for identifying synergistic compound interactions in BACE1 inhibition. The identified ligand pairs may pave the way for novel combination therapies in Alzheimer's disease treatment, offering a promising alternative to current single-drug phase-III inhibitors that exhibit insufficient efficacy and side effects.

Abstract: Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by memory loss, cognitive decline, and behavioral changes, primarily affecting the elderly. Current treatments focus on symptom management as no therapies effectively halt or reverse disease progression. Newer therapies targeting BACE1, a key enzyme in amyloid plaque formation, have shown promise in clinical trials, though they have been limited by side effects and insufficient efficacy in slowing cognitive decline. This is the first study where multi-ligand simultaneous docking (MLSD) was employed to identify potential synergistic inhibitor combinations from among thousands of small molecules that could yield better results than the current phase-III inhibitors of BACE1. The recent phase III drugs such as Atabecestat, Elenbecestat, Lanabecestat, and Verubecestat were considered to be standards for comparison in this study. A library of small molecules with known IC50 values against BACE1 was filtered for flagging Pan-Assay Interference (PAINS) and "Brenk" compounds. Single-ligand docking revealed individual ligand interactions with the protein, forming the basis for creating ligand pairs tested in MLSD. Among these combinations, CHEMBL4078427 and CHEMBL3656158, CHEMBL4078427 and CHEMBL3695732, Verubecestat and CHEMBL3656158, and CHEMBL4078427 and Lanabecestat demonstrated excellent binding affinity of -19.90 kcal/mol, -18.45 kcal/mol, -18.07 kcal/mol, and -17.67 kcal/mol with the protein, which is significantly greater than those of phase-III inhibitors, along with inter-ligand interactions indicative of a synergistic effect. These findings underscore the transformative potential of MLSD in identifying synergistic compound interactions, paving the way for novel combination therapies in the treatment of Alzheimer's disease.

### [4] [The physical foundation beneath protein generative modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04465)
*Tianyu Lu,Po-Ssu Huang*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: This review argues that while generative models have replaced explicit physical equations in protein design, physics still plays a crucial role. The authors contend that physically grounded learned representations and integration of experimental data are foundational for functional protein design, as purely data-driven models can learn non-physical features and fail in out-of-distribution settings.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Generative models trained directly on structure data have made de novo protein sampling efficient, but they can learn non-physical features and break down in out-of-distribution settings typical of protein design campaigns. The paper aims to highlight where physics persists in these pipelines and what issues arise when physically relevant components are unmodeled.

Method: This is a perspective/review paper that surveys where physics remains embedded in protein generative modeling pipelines, examines failure modes when physics is ignored, and proposes that physically grounded learned representations combined with experimental data updating are essential directions forward.

Result: The paper identifies gaps in current generative protein models due to unmodeled physical components and advocates for integrating physics through grounded representations and experimental feedback to enable robust functional protein design.

Conclusion: Respecting underlying macromolecular physics—increasingly via physically grounded learned representations and experimental data updating—is foundational for generative modeling aimed at functional protein design.

Abstract: Physical equations in protein modeling appear to have been replaced by generative models trained directly on structure data. By learning a mapping from noise to data, sampling de novo protein structures has become much more efficient. However, such models can also learn non-physical features and break down with out-of-distribution settings which are typical in protein design campaigns. In this review, we highlight where the physics persists in protein generative modeling pipelines and the issues that linger when physically relevant components of a macromolecular system are left unmodeled. We present a perspective that respecting the underlying physics of macromolecular systems, increasingly through learned representations that are physically grounded and updating generative models with experimental data, is foundational to generative modeling for functional protein design.

### [5] [MarkerScout: A Disease-Agnostic Machine Learning Framework for Biomarker Prediction from Multi-Scale Mechanistic Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04268)
*Robert Moore,Frank Agayie-Ntim,Lindsey B. Crawford,M. Jana Broadhurst,David M. Brett-Major,Prakash Packrisamy,Ahmed Abdeen Hamed,Tomas Helikar*

Main category: q-bio.OT

TL;DR: MarkerScout is a framework for identifying tiered, direction-aware biomarkers across multiple infectious diseases, applied to SARS-CoV-2, Influenza A Virus, and Plasmodium falciparum across hospitalization and ICU cohorts. It achieves cross-validated macro F1 scores from 0.82 to 0.99 and outperforms 94.4% of random feature sets on external ICU data for SARS-CoV-2.

The framework operates through a companion mechanistic immune-simulation platform, evaluating biomarkers across six cohorts (three diseases × two care settings). Key findings include IL-18 as a strong, consistently directed biomarker across both SARS-CoV-2 phases. External validation on independent clinical ICU datasets confirms the top-ranked features' superiority over random feature sets across all three diseases.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a robust framework (MarkerScout) for discovering and validating disease-specific biomarkers that are direction-aware (i.e., indicating whether a biomarker is up- or down-regulated) and tiered by importance, across multiple infectious diseases and clinical phases, with external validation against independent datasets to ensure generalizability.

Method: The framework integrates a mechanistic immune-simulation platform as the foundation, applies feature ranking/selections across six cohorts (three infectious diseases × two clinical settings: hospitalization and ICU), produces tiered biomarker lists with directional information per disease and phase, and validates top-ranked features on external independent ICU datasets by comparing their performance against randomly selected feature sets of equivalent size.

  When benchmarked against three separate, independently collected clinical ICU datasets, MarkerScout's top-ranked features outperformed 94.4% of randomly selected feature sets of equivalent size for SARS-CoV-2, with a weaker but directionally consistent advantage for Influenza A Virus (66.7%) and Plasmodium falciparum (60.7%).

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: This perspective paper discusses the emerging field of Magnetoencephalography (MEG) foundation models, which aim to move brain-signal analysis beyond task-specific pipelines toward reusable models pretrained on broad neural datasets. It provides a didactic overview of key design choices (tokenization, sensor/source space, geometry encoding, architectures, objectives, data) and offers a roadmap for future development including native MEG pretraining, EEG foundation model adaptation, transfer from generic time-series models, and multi-modal integration. Method: The paper is a perspective/review that (1) establishes foundational concepts for understanding MEG foundation models, (2) provides a didactic overview of key design choices including tokenization, sensor- vs source-space representations, sensor-geometry encoding, backbone architectures, self-supervised objectives, and pretraining data, and (3) outlines a roadmap organized around native MEG pretraining, EEG foundation model adaptation, generic time-series transfer, and multi-modal integration.

Abstract: Foundation models are beginning to reshape brain-signal analysis by moving the field beyond task-specific decoding pipelines toward reusable models pretrained on broad neural datasets.
