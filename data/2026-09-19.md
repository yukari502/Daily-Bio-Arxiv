### [1] [Self-Replicating Neural Cellular Automata: Quantifying Emergent Phenotypic and Genotypic Diversity in an OpenEnded Substrate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19902)
*Sanyam Jain,Felix Simon Reimers,Stefano Nichele*

Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: This paper studies a cellular-automata substrate where each pixel hosts a tiny neural network agent that replicates via cloning with mutation, and introduces coarse-grained diversity metrics to measure the resulting ecosystem at phenotypic and genotypic scales.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Growth of self-replicating neural agents in a spatial substrate is hard to measure in terms of diversity; the authors want metrics that capture ecosystem organization at multiple scales.

Method: A two-channel cellular-automata grid where each cell contains a small neural network sensing its Moore neighborhood. Cells persist only by self-replication: a living neighbor is cloned and its weights mutated by uniform perturbation, so phenotype (cell state) is driven by genotype (weights). Four phenotypic metrics (cell-type frequency, entropy, cell variance) and two genotypic metrics (full-weight-vector hash coloring vs. sparse random-weight probe) are evaluated over 1680 small runs (five-fold sweep) and 24 long runs (1000 generations, 200x200).

Result: The substrate is persistent and self-maintaining in 20 of 24 long configurations, forming spatially organized ecosystems of coexisting, competing, and dominating species. A clear phenotype-genotype diversity trade-off emerges: raising phenotypic diversity collapses genotypic diversity and vice versa. Full-genome hash coloring reveals lineage structure that the random-weight probe systematically misses.

Conclusion: The proposed coarse-grained diversity metrics successfully make growth measurable at two scales, expose a phenotype-genotype diversity trade-off, and show that full-genome coloring captures lineage structure better than sparse probes. Code, data, and animations are released as supplementary material.

Abstract: We study an in-silico substrate in which every pixel of a two-channel cellular-automata grid carries a tiny neural network (an agent) that senses its Moore neighborhood. A cell persists only by self-replication: a living neighbor is cloned and its weights are mutated by a uniform perturbation, so that phenotype (cell state) is driven entirely by genotype (network weights). From a handful of seeded founders the system grows into a spatially organized ecosystem of coexisting, competing and dominating species. Our main contribution is a battery of coarse-grained diversity metrics that make such growth measurable at two scales: four phenotypic tools based on cellular-type frequency, entropy and cell variance, and two genotypic tools that colour each agent by a hash of its full weight vector versus a sparse random-weight probe. Across a five-fold sweep of 1680 small runs and 24 long (1000-generation, 200 x 200) runs, the substrate is persistent and self-maintaining in 20 of the 24 long configurations and exposes a clear phenotype-genotype diversity trade-off: raising phenotypic diversity collapses genotypic diversity and vice versa. Full-genome hash colouring further reveals lineage structure that a random-weight probe systematically misses. Code, data and animations are released as supplementary material.

### [2] [Flash-Radiomics: A Scalable Hybrid CPU-CUDA Engine for Standardized Scalar Radiomics and Accelerated Spatial Mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19190)
*Shanli Ding,Yiyi Hu,Ziyu Fu,Chia-Hsin Lin,Ruihan Luo,Jaehee Chun,Xinyue Zhang,Osama Mawlawi*

Main category: q-bio.OT

TL;DR: Flash-Radiomics is a new radiomics tool with CPU and hybrid CUDA backends, providing scalar extraction and spatial mapping with IBSI compliance, concordant results across backends, HDF5 storage, and significant speed advantages over MIRP and PyRadiomics.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Spatial mapping preserves the spatial distribution of radiomic features, but its use is limited by high computational cost and fragmented software. There is a need for a unified tool that is both standardized and computationally efficient.

Method: The authors developed Flash-Radiomics with scalar extraction and spatial mapping, a CPU backend, a hybrid CUDA backend, consistent feature names, and HDF5 storage. They evaluated IBSI compliance (825 IBSI-1 tests, 323 IBSI-2 tests), CPU-CUDA concordance (1,148 scalar pairs, 93 spatial-map pairs), and end-to-end processing time across VOI sizes, comparing against MIRP and PyRadiomics.

Result: Both backends passed all 1,148 IBSI tests with concordant paired results. At the largest scalar input, CPU took 76.343 s vs hybrid CUDA 81.915 s; CPU was 4.7x faster than MIRP and 190.7x faster than PyRadiomics. At the largest spatial input, hybrid CUDA reduced time by 68.6% over CPU (79.280 vs 252.791 s) and was 84.9x faster than PyRadiomics at its largest completed spatial input.

Conclusion: Flash-Radiomics unifies standardized scalar extraction, spatial mapping, concordant CPU-CUDA results, and HDF5 storage. CPU was similar or faster for scalar extraction, while hybrid CUDA was faster for spatial mapping under tested conditions.

Abstract: Background and Objectives: Spatial mapping retains the spatial distribution of radiomic features, but computational cost and fragmented software limit its use. We developed Flash-Radiomics with scalar extraction and spatial mapping, a central processing unit (CPU) backend, a hybrid Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) backend, consistent feature names, and Hierarchical Data Format version 5 (HDF5) storage. Methods: We evaluated Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative (IBSI) compliance, CPU-CUDA concordance, and end-to-end processing time. Compliance testing included 825 chapter 1 (IBSI-1) tests covering 165 high-consensus features and 323 chapter 2 (IBSI-2) tests with numerical references. Concordance testing included 1,148 scalar pairs and 93 spatial-map pairs. End-to-end processing time was measured five times per input volume of interest (VOI) size. Comparisons included the Medical Image Radiomics Processor (MIRP) and PyRadiomics for 102 shared scalar features and PyRadiomics for 93 shared spatial maps. Results: Both backends passed all 1,148 IBSI tests, and all paired results were concordant. At the largest scalar input, CPU required 76.343 s and hybrid CUDA 81.915 s; CPU was 4.7 times faster than MIRP and 190.7 times faster than PyRadiomics. At the largest spatial input completed by both backends, hybrid CUDA reduced processing time by 68.6% relative to CPU (79.280 versus 252.791 s). At PyRadiomics' largest completed spatial input, hybrid CUDA was 84.9 times faster. Conclusions: Flash-Radiomics unified standardized scalar extraction, spatial mapping, concordant CPU-CUDA results, and HDF5 storage. CPU processing time was similar or shorter for scalar extraction, whereas hybrid CUDA was faster for spatial mapping under the tested conditions.

### [3] [A Mathematical Model of Motivated Emotional Mind - Cognitive Embodied System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20437)
*Wiesław L. Galus,Janusz A. Starzyk*

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: The paper formalizes a "Motivated Emotional Mind" cognitive architecture using motivated learning (ML), a generalized reinforcement-learning approach that maintains homeostasis in embodied systems through internal motivations, needs, and affect.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Standard reinforcement-learning models inadequately capture how embodied agents select responses under regulatory pressure, since they neglect need thresholds, goal generation/shifting, bodily state, resource constraints, and action uncertainty.

Method: A mathematical model is developed to formalize a re-entrant loop integrating feedforward processing, lateral interactions, and feedback pathways, plus representational selection mechanisms. Exteroceptive/interoceptive signals, bodily-motivational context, and memory traces bind into associative memory structures called "semblions," which compete for further processing and top-down reconstruction. The formalization covers secondary perception, representational competition, curiosity, procedural gaps, and action selection aimed at limiting allostatic violations. Global affect acts as a central control signal modulating learning rate, representational valence, and the exploration-exploitation balance.

Result: The framework yields a rigorous formalization of motivated learning tailored to embodied systems whose dynamics are shaped by needs, affect, and regulatory state, providing a more adequate account of response selection than standard RL.

Conclusion: This work is a step toward rigorous formalization of cognitive phenomena and may serve as a basis for further theoretical analysis, computer simulation, and implementation in biologically inspired AI systems.

Abstract: This article presents a mathematical model of the Motivated Emotional Mind cognitive architecture developed for embodied intelligent systems. Such a system learns to maintain its homeostasis through a generalized form of reinforcement learning based on its internal motivations, termed motivated learning (ML). The principal contribution of this article is a rigorous formalization of the re-entrant loop integrating feedforward processing, lateral interactions, and feedback pathways, together with the representational selection mechanisms that govern adaptive system responses. The model specifies how ongoing exteroceptive and interoceptive signals, bodily-motivational context, and memory traces are bound into associative memory structures termed semblions, which compete for access to further processing and top-down reconstruction. The formalization encompasses secondary perception, representational competition, curiosity, procedural gaps, and action selection directed toward limiting allostatic violations. Within this framework, motivated learning is tailored to embodied systems whose dynamics are shaped by needs, affect, and the current regulatory state. Unlike standard reinforcement-learning models, the proposed approach incorporates need thresholds, goal generation and shifting goals, bodily state, resource constraints, and action uncertainty, thereby providing a more adequate account of response selection under regulatory pressure. Global affect functions as a central control signal, modulating the learning rate, representational valence, and the balance between exploration and exploitation. The model presented here is a step toward a more rigorous formalization of cognitive phenomena and may provide a basis for further theoretical analysis, computer simulation, and implementation in artificial-intelligence systems inspired by biological processes.

### [4] [Large Language Model Agents for Evidence Based Genetic Disease Severity Classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19569)
*Tohid Ghasemnejad,Ahmadreza Argha,Mark Grosser,John Wang,Min Yang,Thantrira Porntaveetus,Tony Roscioli,Nigel H. Lovell,Mahmoud Aarabi,Hamid Alinejad-Rokny*

Main category: q-bio.GN

TL;DR: An autonomous ReAct+RAG AI agent classifies 10,211 HPO terms by disease severity, achieving 93.55% phenotype-level accuracy and 95.2% external concordance, enabling standardized genomic panel design.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Disease severity classification for genetic conditions is subjective and labor-intensive, creating bottlenecks in genomic screening where commercial panels vary widely in size and overlap.

Method: Integrates ReAct reasoning with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to classify 10,211 HPO terms, using ACMG-endorsed severity guidelines and ACOG quality-of-life criteria to retrieve PubMed literature, generate interpretable reasoning chains, and self-verify claims. Gene-level severity is aggregated across 8,738 pairs.

Result: Phenotype-level accuracy 93.55% (MCC 0.9237) with 82.6–91.4% of claims supported by direct evidence or valid inferences; 3,283 autosomal recessive pairs with severe/profound presentations identified; 95.2% concordance with Mackenzie's Mission gene list.

Conclusion: The system enables standardized panel design through reliable, automated severity classification supported by direct evidence.

Abstract: Disease severity classification for genetic conditions is subjective and labor-intensive, creating bottlenecks in genomic screening, where commercial panels vary widely in size and overlap. We developed an autonomous AI agent integrating Reasoning and Acting (ReAct) with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to classify 10,211 Human Phenotype Ontology terms. It uses American College of Medical Genetics (ACMG)-endorsed severity guidelines and American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) quality-of-life criteria to retrieve PubMed literature, generate interpretable reasoning chains, and independently verify claims. At the phenotype level, using expert-curated cohorts, the agent achieved 93.55% accuracy (MCC 0.9237) with 82.6% to 91.4% of claims supported by direct evidence or valid inferences. Gene-level severity was aggregated across 8,738 pairs, identifying 3,283 autosomal recessive pairs with severe or profound presentations. External validation showed 95.2% concordance with Mackenzie's Mission gene list. This system enables standardized panel design by providing reliable, automated classification supported by direct evidence.

### [5] [A Combined ODE Model of Carbohydrate Fermentation and Colorectal Cancer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19371)
*Alexandra Lawryshyn,Hermann J. Eberl,Thomas Hillen*

Main category: q-bio.TO

TL;DR: A nonlinear ODE model links butyrate from fiber-fermenting gut microbiota to colorectal cancer cells and host cell populations, showing butyrate suppresses tumour growth and that sufficiently intense noise yields almost-sure cancer elimination.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the metabolic and immunological interactions between butyrate-producing gut microbiota, colorectal cancer cells, and host cells, given the role of fiber fermentation and microbial dysbiosis in cancer dynamics.

Method: Formulate and analyze a system of nonlinear ODEs describing butyrate, cancer cells, and host cell populations. Study the model independently and jointly with an existing carbohydrate fermentation model. Explore parameter space via sensitivity analyses and run simulation experiments under varying butyrate availability, including stochastic noise.

Result: Butyrate production is driven by fiber consumption and supported by probiotics under microbial dysbiosis. Butyrate may help suppress tumour growth. Adding sufficiently high-intensity noise leads to almost-sure cancer elimination in infinite time, and this noise threshold decreases as butyrate concentration increases.

Conclusion: The model highlights butyrate's role in limiting colorectal tumour growth and shows that stochastic effects can guarantee cancer elimination, with higher butyrate lowering the required noise intensity.

Abstract: We formulate and analyze a system of non-linear ordinary differential equations that describe key metabolic and immunological interactions between butyrate produced by fiber-fermenting gut microbiota, colorectal cancer cells and host cell populations. The model is studied both independently and in conjunction with a pre-existing carbohydrate fermentation model. The parameter space is explored through sensitivity analyses. Simulation experiments are conducted to illustrate the emergence of varying dynamical behaviour driven by butyrate availability. Our model predicts that butyrate production is driven by fiber consumption and further supported by probiotics in the case of microbial dysbiosis. It also suggests that butyrate may help in suppressing tumour growth. We also show that by adding noise with sufficiently high intensity, cancer elimination occurs almost surely in infinite time and that this threshold level of noise intensity decreases with increasing butyrate concentrations.

</details>
