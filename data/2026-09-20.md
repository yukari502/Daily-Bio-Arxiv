### [1] [A Combined ODE Model of Carbohydrate Fermentation and Colorectal Cancer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19371)
*Alexandra Lawryshyn,Hermann J. Eberl,Thomas Hillen*

Main category: q-bio.TO

TL;DR: A nonlinear ODE model captures metabolic and immunological interactions between butyrate from fiber-fermenting gut microbiota, colorectal cancer cells, and host cell populations. Results indicate butyrate production is driven by fiber (aided by probiotics during dysbiosis), may suppress tumor growth, and that sufficiently intense noise can eliminate cancer almost surely.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how butyrate produced by fiber-fermenting gut microbiota interacts metabolically and immunologically with colorectal cancer cells and host cell populations, and how butyrate availability shapes tumor dynamics.

Method: A system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations describing butyrate, colorectal cancer cells, and host cell populations, studied alone and coupled with a pre-existing carbohydrate fermentation model. Parameter space is explored via sensitivity analyses, simulation experiments illustrate dynamics driven by butyrate availability, and stochastic noise is added to study cancer elimination.

Result: Butyrate production is driven by fiber consumption and further supported by probiotics under microbial dysbiosis. Butyrate may help suppress tumor growth. Adding noise of sufficiently high intensity leads to cancer elimination almost surely in infinite time, and this threshold noise intensity decreases as butyrate concentrations increase.

Conclusion: The model links diet-derived butyrate and probiotic support to tumor suppression, and shows that increasing butyrate lowers the noise intensity needed for almost-sure cancer elimination, suggesting butyrate has a protective, stabilizing role against colorectal cancer.

Abstract: We formulate and analyze a system of non-linear ordinary differential equations that describe key metabolic and immunological interactions between butyrate produced by fiber-fermenting gut microbiota, colorectal cancer cells and host cell populations. The model is studied both independently and in conjunction with a pre-existing carbohydrate fermentation model. The parameter space is explored through sensitivity analyses. Simulation experiments are conducted to illustrate the emergence of varying dynamical behaviour driven by butyrate availability. Our model predicts that butyrate production is driven by fiber consumption and further supported by probiotics in the case of microbial dysbiosis. It also suggests that butyrate may help in suppressing tumour growth. We also show that by adding noise with sufficiently high intensity, cancer elimination occurs almost surely in infinite time and that this threshold level of noise intensity decreases with increasing butyrate concentrations.

### [2] [A Mathematical Model of Motivated Emotional Mind - Cognitive Embodied System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20437)
*Wiesław L. Galus,Janusz A. Starzyk*

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: This paper formalizes the Motivated Emotional Mind architecture for embodied AI, modeling motivated learning via a re-entrant feedforward/lateral/feedback loop with "semblions" and affect-driven control.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Embodied intelligent systems need to maintain homeostasis, but standard reinforcement learning inadequately models systems shaped by needs, affect, and regulatory state.

Method: A mathematical model of a re-entrant loop integrating feedforward processing, lateral interactions, and feedback pathways, plus representational selection. Exteroceptive/interoceptive signals, motivational context, and memory bind into associative structures called semblions that compete for processing. Covers secondary perception, representational competition, curiosity, procedural gaps, and action selection; affect modulates learning rate, valence, and exploration/exploitation.

Result: A rigorous formalization incorporating need thresholds, goal generation/shifting, bodily state, resource constraints, and action uncertainty—providing a more adequate account of response selection under regulatory pressure than standard RL.

Conclusion: The model advances rigorous formalization of cognitive phenomena and offers a basis for theoretical analysis, computer simulation, and biologically-inspired AI implementation.

Abstract: This article presents a mathematical model of the Motivated Emotional Mind cognitive architecture developed for embodied intelligent systems. Such a system learns to maintain its homeostasis through a generalized form of reinforcement learning based on its internal motivations, termed motivated learning (ML). The principal contribution of this article is a rigorous formalization of the re-entrant loop integrating feedforward processing, lateral interactions, and feedback pathways, together with the representational selection mechanisms that govern adaptive system responses. The model specifies how ongoing exteroceptive and interoceptive signals, bodily-motivational context, and memory traces are bound into associative memory structures termed semblions, which compete for access to further processing and top-down reconstruction. The formalization encompasses secondary perception, representational competition, curiosity, procedural gaps, and action selection directed toward limiting allostatic violations. Within this framework, motivated learning is tailored to embodied systems whose dynamics are shaped by needs, affect, and the current regulatory state. Unlike standard reinforcement-learning models, the proposed approach incorporates need thresholds, goal generation and shifting goals, bodily state, resource constraints, and action uncertainty, thereby providing a more adequate account of response selection under regulatory pressure. Global affect functions as a central control signal, modulating the learning rate, representational valence, and the balance between exploration and exploitation. The model presented here is a step toward a more rigorous formalization of cognitive phenomena and may provide a basis for further theoretical analysis, computer simulation, and implementation in artificial-intelligence systems inspired by biological processes.

### [3] [Large Language Model Agents for Evidence Based Genetic Disease Severity Classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19569)
*Tohid Ghasemnejad,Ahmadreza Argha,Mark Grosser,John Wang,Min Yang,Thantrira Porntaveetus,Tony Roscioli,Nigel H. Lovell,Mahmoud Aarabi,Hamid Alinejad-Rokny*

Main category: q-bio.GN

TL;DR: An autonomous ReAct+RAG AI agent classifies disease severity for 10,211 HPO terms, achieving 93.55% accuracy and enabling standardized genomic panel design.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Disease severity classification for genetic conditions is subjective and labor-intensive, causing bottlenecks in genomic screening where commercial panels vary widely in size and overlap.

Method: Integrates ReAct (Reasoning and Acting) with Retrieval-Augmented Generation to classify 10,211 HPO terms, using ACMG-endorsed severity guidelines and ACOG quality-of-life criteria to retrieve PubMed literature, generate interpretable reasoning chains, and independently verify claims.

Result: Achieved 93.55% accuracy (MCC 0.9237) at phenotype level with 82.6%–91.4% of claims supported by direct evidence or valid inferences; gene-level severity aggregated across 8,738 pairs, identifying 3,283 autosomal recessive pairs with severe/profound presentations; external validation showed 95.2% concordance with Mackenzie's Mission gene list.

Conclusion: The system enables standardized panel design by providing reliable, automated severity classification supported by direct evidence.

Abstract: Disease severity classification for genetic conditions is subjective and labor-intensive, creating bottlenecks in genomic screening, where commercial panels vary widely in size and overlap. We developed an autonomous AI agent integrating Reasoning and Acting (ReAct) with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to classify 10,211 Human Phenotype Ontology terms. It uses American College of Medical Genetics (ACMG)-endorsed severity guidelines and American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) quality-of-life criteria to retrieve PubMed literature, generate interpretable reasoning chains, and independently verify claims. At the phenotype level, using expert-curated cohorts, the agent achieved 93.55% accuracy (MCC 0.9237) with 82.6% to 91.4% of claims supported by direct evidence or valid inferences. Gene-level severity was aggregated across 8,738 pairs, identifying 3,283 autosomal recessive pairs with severe or profound presentations. External validation showed 95.2% concordance with Mackenzie's Mission gene list. This system enables standardized panel design by providing reliable, automated classification supported by direct evidence.

### [4] [Flash-Radiomics: A Scalable Hybrid CPU-CUDA Engine for Standardized Scalar Radiomics and Accelerated Spatial Mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19190)
*Shanli Ding,Yiyi Hu,Ziyu Fu,Chia-Hsin Lin,Ruihan Luo,Jaehee Chun,Xinyue Zhang,Osama Mawlawi*

Main category: q-bio.OT

TL;DR: Flash-Radiomics is a radiomics toolkit with scalar extraction and spatial mapping, offering CPU and hybrid CUDA backends, consistent feature names, and HDF5 storage. It passed all 1,148 IBSI compliance tests with concordant CPU-CUDA results, and showed faster processing times than MIRP and PyRadiomics.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Spatial mapping preserves the spatial distribution of radiomic features, but its use is limited by computational cost and fragmented software.

Method: Evaluated IBSI compliance (825 IBSI-1 tests for 165 high-consensus features; 323 IBSI-2 tests), CPU-CUDA concordance (1,148 scalar pairs; 93 spatial-map pairs), and end-to-end processing time (measured five times per VOI size). Compared against MIRP and PyRadiomics for 102 shared scalar features and PyRadiomics for 93 shared spatial maps.

Result: Both backends passed all 1,148 IBSI tests with all paired results concordant. At largest scalar input, CPU took 76.343 s vs hybrid CUDA 81.915 s; CPU was 4.7x faster than MIRP and 190.7x faster than PyRadiomics. At largest spatial input completed by both backends, hybrid CUDA cut time by 68.6% vs CPU (79.280 vs 252.791 s). At PyRadiomics' largest completed spatial input, hybrid CUDA was 84.9x faster.

Conclusion: Flash-Radiomics unifies standardized scalar extraction, spatial mapping, concordant CPU-CUDA results, and HDF5 storage. CPU time was similar or shorter for scalar extraction, while hybrid CUDA was faster for spatial mapping under tested conditions.

Abstract: Background and Objectives: Spatial mapping retains the spatial distribution of radiomic features, but computational cost and fragmented software limit its use. We developed Flash-Radiomics with scalar extraction and spatial mapping, a central processing unit (CPU) backend, a hybrid Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) backend, consistent feature names, and Hierarchical Data Format version 5 (HDF5) storage. Methods: We evaluated Image Biomarker Standardisation Initiative (IBSI) compliance, CPU-CUDA concordance, and end-to-end processing time. Compliance testing included 825 chapter 1 (IBSI-1) tests covering 165 high-consensus features and 323 chapter 2 (IBSI-2) tests with numerical references. Concordance testing included 1,148 scalar pairs and 93 spatial-map pairs. End-to-end processing time was measured five times per input volume of interest (VOI) size. Comparisons included the Medical Image Radiomics Processor (MIRP) and PyRadiomics for 102 shared scalar features and PyRadiomics for 93 shared spatial maps. Results: Both backends passed all 1,148 IBSI tests, and all paired results were concordant. At the largest scalar input, CPU required 76.343 s and hybrid CUDA 81.915 s; CPU was 4.7 times faster than MIRP and 190.7 times faster than PyRadiomics. At the largest spatial input completed by both backends, hybrid CUDA reduced processing time by 68.6% relative to CPU (79.280 versus 252.791 s). At PyRadiomics' largest completed spatial input, hybrid CUDA was 84.9 times faster. Conclusions: Flash-Radiomics unified standardized scalar extraction, spatial mapping, concordant CPU-CUDA results, and HDF5 storage. CPU processing time was similar or shorter for scalar extraction, whereas hybrid CUDA was faster for spatial mapping under the tested conditions.

### [5] [Self-Replicating Neural Cellular Automata: Quantifying Emergent Phenotypic and Genotypic Diversity in an OpenEnded Substrate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19902)
*Sanyam Jain,Felix Simon Reimers,Stefano Nichele*

Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: A two-channel cellular automata grid where each cell hosts a tiny neural network (agent) that replicates by cloning and mutating neighbors. The authors introduce coarse-grained phenotypic and genotypic diversity metrics revealing an ecosystem of coexisting species and a phenotype-genotype diversity trade-off.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To make growth of a neural-network-based artificial substrate measurable across scales, since phenotype (cell state) is driven by genotype (network weights) and existing tools lack metrics to quantify this emergent diversity.

Method: Each pixel of a two-channel cellular automata grid carries a tiny neural network sensing its Moore neighborhood. Cells persist only via self-replication: a living neighbor is cloned with weights mutated by uniform perturbation. Diversity is measured via four phenotypic metrics (cellular-type frequency, entropy, cell variance) and two genotypic metrics (full weight-vector hash coloring vs sparse random-weight probe).

Result: Across a five-fold sweep of 1680 small runs and 24 long (1000-generation, 200x200) runs, the substrate is persistent and self-maintaining in 20 of 24 long configurations. A clear phenotype-genotype diversity trade-off appears: raising phenotypic diversity collapses genotypic diversity and vice versa. Full-genome hash coloring reveals lineage structure that the random-weight probe systematically misses.

Conclusion: The substrate grows into a spatially organized ecosystem of coexisting, competing, and dominating species. The proposed coarse-grained metrics successfully expose emergent diversity dynamics and a fundamental phenotype-genotype trade-off, with full-genome hashing outperforming sparse probes for lineage detection.

Abstract: We study an in-silico substrate in which every pixel of a two-channel cellular-automata grid carries a tiny neural network (an agent) that senses its Moore neighborhood. A cell persists only by self-replication: a living neighbor is cloned and its weights are mutated by a uniform perturbation, so that phenotype (cell state) is driven entirely by genotype (network weights). From a handful of seeded founders the system grows into a spatially organized ecosystem of coexisting, competing and dominating species. Our main contribution is a battery of coarse-grained diversity metrics that make such growth measurable at two scales: four phenotypic tools based on cellular-type frequency, entropy and cell variance, and two genotypic tools that colour each agent by a hash of its full weight vector versus a sparse random-weight probe. Across a five-fold sweep of 1680 small runs and 24 long (1000-generation, 200 x 200) runs, the substrate is persistent and self-maintaining in 20 of the 24 long configurations and exposes a clear phenotype-genotype diversity trade-off: raising phenotypic diversity collapses genotypic diversity and vice versa. Full-genome hash colouring further reveals lineage structure that a random-weight probe systematically misses. Code, data and animations are released as supplementary material.

</details>
