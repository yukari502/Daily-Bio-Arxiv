### [1] [Grid-cell firing fields lack local sixfold symmetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31145)
*Anian Kerscher*

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: Individual grid-cell firing fields are locally circular/radially symmetric, showing no detectable sixfold angular modulation despite the global hexagonal lattice organization.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: It remains unclear whether individual grid fields are radially symmetric or inherit weak sixfold modulation from the global hexagonal lattice. Resolving this constrains grid-field microstructure and models of grid formation.

Method: Harmonic analysis of within-field angular structure, using per-cell matched simulations preserving sampling statistics, field scale, and lattice geometry after correcting for global elliptic deformation. Supported by a sixfold regression metric, harmonic leave-one-out sensitivity, robustness checks (radial window, smoothing, noise), and an observation-matched Burak--Fiete continuous-attractor network.

Result: Experimental sixfold harmonic power fractions matched locally circular reference fields and were far below simulations with imposed local sixfold modulation. The Burak--Fiete network showed no selective local sixfold enhancement even when scale and relative field width were matched.

Conclusion: Global hexagonal lattice organization need not produce detectable local sixfold angular modulation of individual fields, placing quantitative constraints on grid-field microstructure and mechanistic models of grid formation.

Abstract: The firing fields of a grid cell form a hexagonal lattice, yet each field's intrinsic geometric structure remains unclear: Are individual grid fields radially symmetric or do they exhibit (weak) sixfold modulation inherited from the global lattice? To address this question, we quantified the within-field angular structure using harmonic analysis and per-cell matched simulations that preserved sampling statistics, field scale, and lattice geometry after correction for global elliptic deformation. We found that, across the investigated cells, the sixfold harmonic power fraction was consistent with values from matched locally circular reference fields. Experimental fields further showed substantially less sixfold modulation than matched simulations with an imposed local sixfold component, demonstrating sensitivity to sixfold structure at that level. This conclusion was supported by a complementary sixfold regression metric, harmonic leave-one-out sensitivity analyses, and robustness checks across radial-window choices, smoothing, and simulated background-noise levels. An observation-matched Burak--Fiete continuous-attractor network likewise showed no selective local sixfold enhancement despite its globally periodic grid structure, and this conclusion persisted when grid scale and relative field width were matched more closely to the experimental regime. These results suggest that the global hexagonal lattice organization need not be accompanied by detectable local sixfold angular modulation of individual firing fields, placing quantitative constraints on grid-field microstructure and mechanistic models of grid formation.

### [2] [Learning a non-linguistic code for inferred rules from reward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31192)
*Cristiano Capone*

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: Two neural networks develop a shared symbolic code that transmits inferable rules to naive learners, even generalizing to unseen three-step transformations, analogous to how aphasia patients communicate via gesture without shared code.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Investigate how a rule learned from examples can be conveyed to someone who never saw those examples, without any pre-existing shared code—as severe aphasia patients do via gesture/sketch.

Method: A speaker network sees worked examples and emits eight invented symbols; a listener network, blind to the examples, applies the symbols to a new input. The speaker is rewarded for listener success and learns a code; new learners then acquire it.

Result: The code carries rules for three-step transformations never seen in training and is learnable by new learners. It shows two regimes: reward alone collapses to one message (like human language word loss), while expressive pressure maintains differentiation. Generalization tracks rule-relevance of messages, not message diversity.

Conclusion: The learning signal shapes the code: reward sorts many rules under few fixed labels, whereas the listener's error gradient assigns each rule a region of similar messages, far better at distinguishing rules.

Abstract: How can a rule inferred from examples reach someone who never saw them, without a shared code? Patients with severe aphasia do it by gesture or sketch. One network sees worked examples and emits eight invented symbols; a second, blind to them, applies them to a new input. Rewarded for the second's success, the first learns a code carrying rules to three-step transformations training never presents, which new learners acquire. Like invented human languages, the code has two regimes: under reward alone the speaker drifts to one message, as human languages lose words under plain transmission; expressive pressure keeps messages differentiated. Success on new rules tracks how much the message says about the rule, not how varied messages are. The learning signal shapes the code: reward sorts many rules under few fixed labels; the listener's error gradient gives each rule a region of similar messages, telling rules apart far better.

### [5] [Prevalence and the rate of antigenic evolution are jointly determined in an eco-evolutionary SIR model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30515)
*Daniel Gomez,Joshua B. Plotkin*

Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: The paper extends Andreasen et al.'s model of influenza A eco-evolutionary dynamics to include infection-induced host mortality, constructing the endemic traveling-pulse solution asymptotically in the weak-mutation limit.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Influenza A's antigenic evolution and infection prevalence are coupled: immunity that selects new variants arises from past infections. Understanding this feedback, and how virulence (host killing) alters it, requires a tractable model of the endemic state.

Method: The authors analyze a model where infected and recovered hosts are distributions over a 1D antigenic space linked by cross-immunity, extending it to allow infection-caused host death. The endemic state is a traveling pulse (fixed shape, constant speed through antigenic space); they construct this pulse asymptotically in the weak-antigenic-mutation limit.

Result: (i) Antigenic evolution speed decreases as prevalence rises. (ii) Less standing antigenic variation accompanies either faster or slower evolution, depending on whether pathogens differ in immune evasion or host lifespan. (iii) Total infected hosts is largest at intermediate virulence when host birth rate is sufficiently low.

Conclusion: The eco-evolutionary feedback produces a traveling-pulse endemic state whose properties link evolutionary speed, antigenic diversity, prevalence, and virulence in non-trivial ways, with virulence maximizing total infections at intermediate levels under low host birth rates.

Abstract: Influenza A persists by continually changing the antigens that its host population recognizes. How fast it evolves and how many hosts it infects are not independent, because the immunity that selects new variants is produced by the infections the pathogen has already caused. We analyze a model of Andreasen et.\ al.\ that captures this eco-evolutionary feedback, and we extend it to the case where infection may kill the host. In the model infected and recovered hosts are distributions over a one-dimensional antigenic space coupled by cross-immunity. The endemic state is a traveling pulse: the infected distribution keeps a fixed shape and advances through antigenic space at constant speed. In the limit of weak antigenic mutation we construct the pulse asymptotically and find that: (i) the speed of antigenic evolution decreases as prevalence rises, (ii) less standing antigenic variation accompanies either faster or slower antigenic evolution, depending on whether pathogens differ in immune evasion or host lifespan (iii) the total number of infected hosts is largest at an intermediate virulence when the host birth rate is sufficiently low.

The single-molecule architecture offers ~6-fold evolutionary advantage over multi-component scenarios by maintaining genetic linkage and retaining catalytic products near the replicating genome (resisting diffusion loss). Resistance to parasitic "cheaters" follows as a corollary.

Conclusion: The riboreplisome framework explains the origins of ribosomal, transfer, and messenger RNA as derivatives of a single ancestral molecule and provides a driving force for cellular compartmentalisation. The riboreplisome framework explains the origins of ribosomal, transfer, and messenger RNA as derivatives of a single ancestral molecule, while providing a driving force for cellular compartmentalisation. Identification or reconstruction of riboreplisome-like molecules could advance both origin-of-life research and synthetic biology applications.

### [7] [Large-deviations theory for growing chemical reaction networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30970)
*Praful Gagrani,Ignacio Madrid,Tetsuya J Kobayashi*

Main category: q-bio.MN

TL;DR: The paper develops a large-deviation theory for exponentially growing stochastic chemical reaction networks, separating intrinsic chemical noise from other variability by decomposing abundance into volume and composition.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Growing biochemical systems are intrinsically noisy due to discrete reaction events. Since growing systems lack stationary abundance distributions, it is unclear how to separate intrinsic chemical noise from other variability in population and single-cell data.

Method: The authors decompose abundance into volume and composition, where balanced growth corresponds to stable composition with exponentially increasing volume. They establish a large-deviation principle for composition fluctuations with speed equal to system volume, with the quasipotential determined by the contact Hamilton-Jacobi equation. They also derive a fluctuation theory for accumulated observables.

Result: Composition fluctuations satisfy a large-deviation principle with speed equal to growing-system volume. Accumulated observable covariances decay with accumulated volume rather than physical time. Applications to a minimal autocatalytic network and a coarse-grained cellular growth model predict composition quasipotentials, growth-rate fluctuations, and correlations between observables.

Conclusion: The framework provides a stochastic null model for single-cell heterogeneity arising from intrinsic stochasticity of the underlying chemical reaction network.

Abstract: Growing biochemical systems are intrinsically noisy, with one important source of stochasticity arising from the discrete reaction events of the underlying biochemical network. Because growing systems do not generally admit stationary abundance distributions, it is unclear how to separate this intrinsic chemical noise from other sources of variability in population and single-cell data. Here we develop a large-deviation theory for exponentially growing stochastic chemical reaction networks by decomposing abundance into volume and composition. In these coordinates, balanced growth corresponds to a stable composition together with exponentially increasing volume. We show that composition fluctuations satisfy a large-deviation principle with speed equal to the volume of the growing system, and the corresponding quasipotential is selected by a solution of the contact Hamilton--Jacobi equation. We also derive a fluctuation theory for accumulated observables and show that their covariances decay with accumulated-volume, rather than with physical time. Applications to a minimal autocatalytic network and a coarse-grained cellular growth model demonstrate how the theory predicts composition quasipotentials, growth-rate fluctuations, and correlations between observables. The framework therefore provides a stochastic null model for single-cell heterogeneity generated by the intrinsic stochasticity of the underlying chemical reaction network.

</details>
