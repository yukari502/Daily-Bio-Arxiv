  <summary>Details</summary>

Finite γ is analyzed via spectral deformation, instability selection, and an oscillatory region below the isolated Hopf threshold; field closure is tested on a dendrite grown at γ=0 then frozen.

Result: Finite γ deforms the spectrum, shifts the selected instability, and opens an oscillatory region below the isolated Hopf threshold. On a frozen γ=0 dendrite, field closure increases distal persistence without changing geometry; the first-order effect is positive across realizations and localizes to a small set of non-cable field shortcuts. Below threshold, closure is nearly the transmission; at spike amplitude it is a correction to a regenerative cable event.

Conclusion: A dendritic tree is not intrinsically a structural spandrel. Shape alone yields morphology without internal state or source term—the "silhouette of the ice neuron."
