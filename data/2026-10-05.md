### [1] [A coupled geometric surface and surface reaction-diffusion framework for modelling nuclear mechanics during confined cell migration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02231)
*Francesca Ballatore,Silvia Comunian,Anotida Madzvamuse,Slimane Ait-Si-Ali,Rachele Allena*

Main category: q-bio.CB

TL;DR: A 2D computational framework couples cell/nuclear membrane mechanics with surface reaction-diffusion of epigenetic markers to study confined migration, applied to lung cancer cells where SETDB1/SUV39H1 balance controls H3K9me3 and nuclear mechanics.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Confined cell migration is governed by nuclear and cellular deformation, but how epigenetic regulation of the nuclear periphery (e.g., H3K9me3 distribution via SETDB1/SUV39H1) feeds back into nuclear mechanics remains poorly captured in a unified model.

Method: A two-dimensional mathematical framework represents cell and nuclear membranes as evolving closed surfaces governed by force-balance laws with surface tension, bending elasticity, and external mechanical interactions. A surface reaction-diffusion system on the evolving nuclear surface describes molecular regulation at the periphery. The framework is applied to an experimentally characterized lung cancer cell model comparing control and SETDB1 knockout conditions, solved numerically via the evolving surface finite element method using experimentally measured mechanical parameters.

Result: Numerical solutions qualitatively reproduce the changes in nuclear mechanics and part of the experimentally observed increase in entry time in SETDB1 KO cells. The model reproduces the direction of experimental observations.

Conclusion: The model captures the qualitative trend of SETDB1 KO effects on nuclear mechanics and confined migration entry time; the residual gap between simulated and measured entry times indicates the cellular response is multifactorial.

Abstract: Confined cell migration is governed by nuclear and cellular deformation. In this work, we formulate a two-dimensional mathematical framework in which the cell and nuclear membranes are represented as evolving closed surfaces governed by force-balance laws incorporating surface tension, bending elasticity, and external mechanical interactions. Nuclear mechanics depends on several factors, including chromatin organization and compaction, which are regulated by epigenetic modifications. Molecular regulation at the nuclear periphery is described by a surface reaction-diffusion system posed on the evolving nuclear surface. We apply this coupled framework to an experimentally characterized lung cancer cell model in which the balance between the enzymes SETDB1 and SUV39H1 regulates the peripheral deposition of H3K9me3. Using experimentally measured mechanical parameters, we represent control (Ctrl) and SETDB1 knockout (SETDB1 KO) conditions, which display distinct H3K9me3 distributions, especially at the nuclear periphery. Numerical solutions obtained using the evolving surface finite element method qualitatively reproduce the changes in nuclear mechanics as well as part of the experimentally observed increase in the entry time in SETDB1 KO cells. The model therefore reproduces the direction of the experimental observations, while the residual difference between simulated and measured entry times indicates that the cellular response is multifactorial.

*A. Mayeux*

Main category: q-bio.NC

TL;DR: The paper constructs binary XOR cycles of certain sizes that form complexes (maximally integrated subsystems) in every state, with integrated information growing doubly-exponentially (2^(quadratic)) in the number of units.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Integrated information theory (IIT) seeks to quantify integration, but explicit examples with extreme integration are rare; the work likely aims to demonstrate that simple binary XOR systems can exhibit vastly growing integrated information and be complexes in all states.

Method: Constructs a cycle of binary units where each unit applies XOR to itself and its two clockwise neighbours, then analyzes integration across states; for infinitely many cycle sizes it is shown to be a complex in every state (no proper subsystem more integrated).

Result: For infinitely many sizes, the constructed cycle is a complex in every state, and its integrated information scales as 2 raised to a power quadratic in the number of units.

Conclusion: Simple XOR-based binary cycles can achieve maximal integration in all states with super-exponentially (doubly-exponentially in exponent form) large integrated information for infinitely many sizes.

Abstract: For infinitely many sizes we exhibit a cycle of binary units, each taking the exclusive-or of itself and its two clockwise neighbours, that is a complex in every state, no proper subsystem being more integrated, and whose integrated information grows as two raised to a power quadratic in the number of units.

  <summary>Details</summary>

Method: The authors tested the meta-criterion on non-cluster-forming dynamical systems with different attractor geometries, examined whether cluster centroids fall in random attractor regions, and applied topological analysis to dynamical systems and resting-state EEG in sensor space.

Abstract: Microstate analysis starts with clustering and the meta-criterion is a heuristic to find an optimum cluster number.   <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Contrastive representation learning is increasingly used to recover low-dimensional structure from neural recordings, yet its output is usually validated by decoding accuracy rather than by the geometry of the learned manifold. Abstract: Contrastive representation learning is increasingly used to recover low-dimensional structure from neural recordings, but its output is typically validated by decoding accuracy rather than by the geometry of the manifold it produces. However, participant-level model does separate from its identity-aware null (p = 0.0099) while speaker-versus-listener role analysis performs at chance in the same embedding, indicating a manifold organized by individual -- and, in contrast with current neurolinguistics models, almost invariant to speaking vs. listening. Based on these results, we suggest that retraining-based nulls should be the default for grouped-data contrastive embeddings.
