Here, we use a survey-derived individual-level exposure framework for 2,279 residents to simulate infection dynamics across ranges of transmission intensity and vaccine efficacy. We compare two vaccination strategies: the high-exposure strategy (prioritizing highly exposed individuals) and the oldest-first strategy (prioritizing older individuals). Overall, our findings highlight the importance of considering vaccine failure mode when evaluating vaccination policies under different transmission dynamics.

Abstract: Neuronal excitability is commonly modeled using different principles at the cellular and population scales, making it difficult to study how microscopic mechanisms shape macroscopic activity. We introduce a spatiotemporal excitatory-inhibitory (EI) model that unifies these scales through a common mixed-feedback architecture. The model retains the conductance-based circuit structure of Hodgkin-Huxley models and describes intrinsic and synaptic currents through memductances whose temporal and spatial voltage dependence encodes dynamics at the relevant scale.
