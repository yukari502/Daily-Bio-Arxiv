<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional source-target attention in Transformers can be a bottleneck for modeling complex interactions, and drug discovery requires more advanced AI methods.

Abstract: The growing sophistication of drug discovery calls for new advances in artificial intelligence (AI). Transformers have underpinned recent breakthroughs in AI, yet their conventional source-target attention mechanism can become a bottleneck for modeling complex interactions. Here, we introduce TopoLIA, a topology-inspired latent interaction attention mechanism that learns not only how much information should be exchanged between interaction partners, but also how that information should be transformed. Source-target-dependent attention is mediated through a compact latent interaction space, providing a structured representation of complex interactions. We further incorporate topological spectral representations to capture multiscale data geometry and extract algebraic relationships. TopoLIA thus incorporates topological principles into both data representation and neural network architecture. Across multiple scoring and docking benchmarks, TopoLIA achieves state-of-the-art performance and consistently outperforms its standard-attention counterpart. Rank analyses show that compact latent dimensions yield the best performance, supporting compact interaction spaces as an effective inductive bias for biomolecular learning. More broadly, LIA provides a general framework for structured, source-target-dependent information exchange that is applicable to diverse scientific and AI problems involving complex heterogeneous interactions.
