Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: fBM's H and D_H are jointly estimated via MLE from density and trajectory data; a neural network approximating the posterior mean of H gives the best RMSE, and vesicle analysis shows intracellular cargoes switch between sub- and superdiffusion over time.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Anomalous diffusion described by fractional Brownian motion requires jointly estimating the Hurst exponent H and the diffusion coefficient D_H. It is unclear whether density measurements alone can infer both parameters, how trajectory-based inference precision depends on the estimator, and whether intracellular cargoes exhibit fixed or time-varying diffusion modes.

Method: Maximum likelihood estimation of H and D_H from density measurements with Poisson counting noise and from single-particle trajectories with Gaussian localisation error. A neural network trained with squared error loss approximates the posterior mean of H, paired with a profile-likelihood estimate of D_H. Inference is also applied to vesicle trajectories from MRC5 fibroblasts and HeLa cells.

Result: A single density measurement in time cannot infer both H and D_H; several time points are needed to determine precision. The neural network approximates the posterior mean of H at negligible cost, and paired with the profile-likelihood estimate of D_H achieves the lowest RMSE for both parameters, except near H=0 and H=1 where MLE estimates H better. In vesicle trajectories, H and D_H form a continuum rather than distinct modes. About 26% of MRC5 fibroblast and 21% of HeLa vesicle trajectories contain both subdiffusive and superdiffusive windows, versus 2% and 7% expected from estimation uncertainty alone.

Conclusion: Joint estimation of H and D_H requires multiple time points for density data. A neural posterior-mean estimator of H with profile-likelihood D_H is generally most accurate, though MLE is better near the H boundaries. Intracellular cargoes switch between subdiffusion and superdiffusion over time rather than belonging to fixed-H populations.

Abstract: Fractional Brownian motion (fBM) is a model of anomalous diffusion parameterised by a Hurst exponent $H \in (0,1)$ and a generalised diffusion coefficient $D_H > 0$.
