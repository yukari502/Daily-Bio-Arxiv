### [1] [Panoramic modelling of single-cell transcriptomes at scale with CellNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21943)
*Xiaoqing Lian,Pei Liu,Jiahui Tan,Luoda Tan,Chaoyi Li,Quan Zou,Chen Lin,Xiangxiang Zeng*

Main category: q-bio.GN

TL;DR: CellNet models single-cell transcriptomes by unifying DNA-derived gene identities with measured RNA expression in a shared image representation, pretrained on 231M cell profiles across 40 species, improving rare/condition-specific cell recognition and enabling cross-species gene expression prediction and non-coding module retrieval.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Comprehensive characterization of cellular diversity is key to studying tissue development and disease, but existing single-cell foundation models capture only a restricted portion of the increasingly rich molecular information revealed by profiling advances.

Method: CellNet integrates DNA-derived gene identities and measured RNA expression into a shared image representation for panoramic modelling of single-cell transcriptomes. It is pretrained on 231 million cell profiles spanning 40 species.

Result: CellNet improves rare-population and condition-associated state recognition while retaining competitive general representations. It predicts withheld coding-gene expression and selected non-coding targets in total-RNA data, recovers lineage-specific spatial expression patterns, generalizes across species (including targets without expression-pretraining exposure), and its gene representations retrieve known non-coding modules and developmental/neural programmes even from sparse RNA queries.

Conclusion: CellNet is a scalable framework for interpreting cellular diversity through increasingly comprehensive transcriptomic measurements.

Abstract: Comprehensively characterizing cellular diversity is critical for understanding tissue development, identifying disease-associated populations and investigating gene programmes linked to specialized functions. Advances in single-cell profiling reveal additional cell-specific transcripts and molecular associations, yet many foundation models represent only a restricted portion of this increasingly rich molecular information. Here we present CellNet, which enables panoramic modelling of single-cell transcriptomes by integrating DNA-derived gene identities and measured RNA expression in a shared image representation. Pretrained on 231 million cell profiles spanning 40 species, CellNet improves rare-population and condition-associated state recognition in evaluated cohorts while retaining competitive general cell representations. It predicts withheld coding-gene expression and selected non-coding targets in total-RNA data, and recovers lineage-specific expression patterns in spatial measurements. Prediction also extends across species, including to targets without expression-pretraining exposure. Its gene representations retrieve known non-coding modules and developmental or neural programmes, including from sparsely detected RNA queries. Together, these results establish CellNet as a scalable framework for interpreting cellular diversity through increasingly comprehensive transcriptomic measurements.

### [2] [Documenting Trofinetide Treatment Phenotypes in Rett Syndrome with a Dual-LLM Consensus Pipeline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22062)
*Daniel Palacios,Guantong Qi,Mikael Guzman Karlsson,Shawntel K. David,Mohammed Sameeruddin,Bernhard Suter,Zhandong Liu,Davut Pehlivan,Hu Chen*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: A dual-LLM consensus pipeline extracts 18 trofinetide treatment-response phenotypes from 26,763 clinical notes of 108 Rett syndrome patients, achieving 84.2% accuracy on a diarrhea development set and 90.2% expert agreement, far outperforming regular-expression matching.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Trofinetide became the first approved Rett syndrome treatment in 2023, but scalable real-world monitoring is blocked because chart review is labor-intensive. Existing keyword/regular-expression extraction cannot reliably capture nuanced, multi-domain treatment responses in free-text notes.

Method: Clinical notes (26,763 from 108 patients with confirmed sustained trofinetide exposure at Texas Children's Hospital) were keyword-filtered, then each note was classified by two LLMs requiring consensus, and results were aggregated into a patient-by-timepoint matrix. Architecture and prompts were tuned on a diarrhea development set (scored on 183 expert-labeled cells), then frozen and applied out-of-sample to 17 other phenotypes (4 adverse effects, 13 Rett clinical features, medication refusal) by substituting phenotype definitions.

Result: A prompt requiring diarrhea to be active and severe performed best at 84.2 ± 0.0% accuracy (F1 = 0.674), 47 percentage points above regex matching (p < 0.0001). Expert reviewers agreed with 90.2% (92/102) of the pipeline's calls across phenotypes. Motor impairment, language/communication, and stereotypy were documented present in 92–100% of patients at every visit.

Conclusion: In-context LLM reasoning coupled with a consensus architecture offers a scalable alternative to keyword and regular-expression extraction for real-world evidence, adapting to new phenotypes and medications through configuration alone. The captured outcome is documented presence at each visit, not a direction-of-change judgment.

Abstract: Objective: Trofinetide, approved in 2023 as the first Rett syndrome treatment, lacks scalable real-world monitoring because chart review is labor-intensive. We developed a dual large language model (LLM) consensus pipeline to extract 18 treatment-response phenotypes, comprising 4 adverse effects, 13 Rett clinical features, and medication refusal, from clinical notes. Materials and Methods: We analyzed 26,763 notes from 108 patients with confirmed sustained trofinetide exposure at Texas Children's Hospital. The pipeline filters notes by keyword, classifies each through two LLMs requiring consensus, and aggregates a patient-by-timepoint matrix. Architecture and prompt were selected on a diarrhea development set, then frozen and applied to the other 17 phenotypes with the phenotype definition substituted, as an out-of-sample test. The reported prompt comparison scores 183 expert-labeled cells. Results: A prompt requiring diarrhea to be active and severe performed best (84.2 $\pm$ 0.0% accuracy; F1 = 0.674), 47 percentage points above regular-expression matching ($p < 0.0001$). Expert reviewers judged six of the pipeline's calls per phenotype, three positive and three negative, and agreed with 90.2% (92/102). The 13 Rett clinical features are manifestations of the disorder itself rather than adverse effects of trofinetide. Gross and fine motor impairment, language/communication, and stereotypy were documented present in 92-100% of patients at every visit. Discussion: In-context LLM extraction captured multi-domain treatment responses. The outcome is documented presence at each visit, not a direction-of-change judgment. Conclusion: Coupling in-context LLM reasoning with a consensus architecture offers a scalable alternative to keyword and regular-expression extraction for real-world evidence, adapting to new phenotypes and medications through configuration alone.

TL;DR: Mental training reshapes non-equilibrium brain dynamics along two dissociable axes: meditation dose reduces pan-cerebral exploratory breadth, while 8-week MBIs bidirectionally tune temporal persistence depending on baseline clinical status.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Time-averaged neural measures collapse the multi-scale, dynamic nature of brain activity, so it remains unclear how behavioural interventions reorganise hierarchical brain function and how such mechanisms differ between healthy and clinically vulnerable populations.

Method: Applied a sliding-window Temporal Irreversibility Index to source-reconstructed EEG in three complementary cohorts (n = 141), decomposing hierarchical brain trajectories into three orthogonal temporal descriptors. A joint Hidden Markov Model linked dynamical shifts to microstate substrates, and longitudinal changes were related to mindfulness facets.

Result: Meditation practice produced a universal, pan-cerebral reduction in exploratory breadth, dose-dependent on lifetime practice hours. Temporal persistence was bidirectionally tuned by 8-week MBIs: reduced in healthy meditators but increased in trauma-exposed individuals. Dynamical shifts mapped to distinct microstate substrates and selectively tracked improvements in attentional presence in daily life.

Conclusion: The findings establish a mechanistic framework for how behavioural training reorganises non-equilibrium brain dynamics, with implications for tailoring mindfulness-based interventions to individual clinical profiles.

Abstract: How mental training reshapes the temporal architecture of large-scale brain dynamics and how these mechanisms differ between healthy and clinically vulnerable populations remains poorly understood. Time-averaged neural measures collapse the multi-scale nature of brain activity, obscuring the dynamic processes through which behavioural interventions reorganise hierarchical brain function. Here, applying a sliding-window Temporal Irreversibility Index to source-reconstructed EEG across three complementary cohorts (n = 141), we decompose hierarchical brain trajectories into three orthogonal temporal descriptors and uncover two dissociable, mechanistically interpretable axes of intervention-induced plasticity. Cumulative meditation practice drives a universal, pan-cerebral reduction in the exploratory breadth of hierarchical dynamics, tracking dose-dependently with lifetime hours of practice. Temporal persistence is bidirectionally tuned by 8-week mindfulness-based interventions in a population-specific way: reduced in healthy meditators but increased in trauma-exposed individuals, revealing that comparable MBI protocols engage qualitatively distinct network mechanisms depending on baseline clinical status. A joint Hidden Markov Model further links these dynamical shifts to distinct microstate substrates, and longitudinal changes selectively track improvements in a single facet of mindfulness, attentional presence in daily life. Together, these findings establish a mechanistic framework for how behavioural training reorganises non-equilibrium brain dynamics, with direct implications for tailoring mindfulness-based interventions to individual clinical profiles.

  <summary>Details</summary>

Method: An ensemble over complete spike histories is constructed, assigning each spatiotemporal spike configuration an effective energy functional measuring consistency with intrinsic synaptic interactions. This yields a Boltzmann-like distribution where an effective temperature represents stochastic external drive. Macroscopic states are described via coarse-grained observables (e.g., population firing-rate densities) emerging from competition between energy and entropy. Regimes are diagnosed with order parameters: entropy, temporal localization measures, and autocorrelation indicators. Monte Carlo Metropolis-Hastings simulations directly on spike trains complement the analysis.

Simulations reproduce the predicted regimes and provide a sampling-based probe of the free-energy landscape.

Abstract: We introduce a statistical-mechanical framework for spiking neuronal systems in which entire spike trains, defined as spatiotemporal configurations of discrete events, are treated as microstates of the system.
