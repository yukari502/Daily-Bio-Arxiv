### [1] [Documenting Trofinetide Treatment Phenotypes in Rett Syndrome with a Dual-LLM Consensus Pipeline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22062)
*Daniel Palacios,Guantong Qi,Mikael Guzman Karlsson,Shawntel K. David,Mohammed Sameeruddin,Bernhard Suter,Zhandong Liu,Davut Pehlivan,Hu Chen*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: A dual-LLM consensus pipeline extracts 18 treatment-response phenotypes from 26,763 clinical notes for trofinetide-treated Rett syndrome patients, beating regex by 47 percentage points and matching expert labels 90.2% of the time.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Trofinetide became the first approved Rett syndrome treatment in 2023, but scalable real-world monitoring is blocked because manual chart review is labor-intensive; keyword/regex extraction is insufficient for nuanced clinical narratives.

Method: Retrospective analysis of 26,763 notes from 108 patients with sustained trofinetide exposure at Texas Children's Hospital. The pipeline keyword-filters notes, classifies each with two LLMs requiring consensus, and aggregates a patient-by-timepoint matrix. Architecture and prompts were tuned on a diarrhea development set, then frozen and applied out-of-sample to 17 other phenotypes (4 adverse effects, 13 Rett features, medication refusal) by swapping phenotype definitions. Prompt comparison scored 183 expert-labeled cells.

Result: The best prompt (diarrhea required to be active and severe) reached 84.2 ± 0.0% accuracy and F1 = 0.674, 47 percentage points above regex matching (p < 0.0001). Experts reviewed 6 calls per phenotype and agreed with 90.2% (92/102). Gross/fine motor impairment, language/communication, and stereotypy were documented in 92-100% of patients at every visit.

Conclusion: In-context LLM reasoning with a consensus architecture offers a scalable alternative to keyword/regex extraction for real-world evidence, generalizing to new phenotypes and medications via configuration alone. Output reflects documented presence at each visit, not direction of change.

Abstract: Objective: Trofinetide, approved in 2023 as the first Rett syndrome treatment, lacks scalable real-world monitoring because chart review is labor-intensive. We developed a dual large language model (LLM) consensus pipeline to extract 18 treatment-response phenotypes, comprising 4 adverse effects, 13 Rett clinical features, and medication refusal, from clinical notes. Materials and Methods: We analyzed 26,763 notes from 108 patients with confirmed sustained trofinetide exposure at Texas Children's Hospital. The pipeline filters notes by keyword, classifies each through two LLMs requiring consensus, and aggregates a patient-by-timepoint matrix. Architecture and prompt were selected on a diarrhea development set, then frozen and applied to the other 17 phenotypes with the phenotype definition substituted, as an out-of-sample test. The reported prompt comparison scores 183 expert-labeled cells. Results: A prompt requiring diarrhea to be active and severe performed best (84.2 $\pm$ 0.0% accuracy; F1 = 0.674), 47 percentage points above regular-expression matching ($p < 0.0001$). Expert reviewers judged six of the pipeline's calls per phenotype, three positive and three negative, and agreed with 90.2% (92/102). The 13 Rett clinical features are manifestations of the disorder itself rather than adverse effects of trofinetide. Gross and fine motor impairment, language/communication, and stereotypy were documented present in 92-100% of patients at every visit. Discussion: In-context LLM extraction captured multi-domain treatment responses. The outcome is documented presence at each visit, not a direction-of-change judgment. Conclusion: Coupling in-context LLM reasoning with a consensus architecture offers a scalable alternative to keyword and regular-expression extraction for real-world evidence, adapting to new phenotypes and medications through configuration alone.

### [2] [Panoramic modelling of single-cell transcriptomes at scale with CellNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21943)
*Xiaoqing Lian,Pei Liu,Jiahui Tan,Luoda Tan,Chaoyi Li,Quan Zou,Chen Lin,Xiangxiang Zeng*

Main category: q-bio.GN

TL;DR: CellNet panoramically models single-cell transcriptomes by mapping DNA-derived gene identities and measured RNA expression into a shared image representation, pretrained on 231M cell profiles across 40 species.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Comprehensive characterization of cellular diversity is key to understanding tissue development, disease-associated populations, and specialized gene programmes, but existing foundation models capture only a restricted portion of increasingly rich single-cell molecular information.

Method: Integrates DNA-derived gene identities with measured RNA expression into a shared image representation, pretrained on 231 million cell profiles spanning 40 species.

Result: Improves rare-population and condition-associated state recognition while keeping competitive general representations; predicts withheld coding-gene and selected non-coding targets in total-RNA data; recovers lineage-specific spatial patterns; generalizes across species (including targets without expression-pretraining exposure); gene representations retrieve known non-coding modules and developmental/neural programmes, even from sparsely detected RNA queries.

Conclusion: CellNet is a scalable framework for interpreting cellular diversity through increasingly comprehensive transcriptomic measurements.

Abstract: Comprehensively characterizing cellular diversity is critical for understanding tissue development, identifying disease-associated populations and investigating gene programmes linked to specialized functions. Advances in single-cell profiling reveal additional cell-specific transcripts and molecular associations, yet many foundation models represent only a restricted portion of this increasingly rich molecular information. Here we present CellNet, which enables panoramic modelling of single-cell transcriptomes by integrating DNA-derived gene identities and measured RNA expression in a shared image representation. Pretrained on 231 million cell profiles spanning 40 species, CellNet improves rare-population and condition-associated state recognition in evaluated cohorts while retaining competitive general cell representations. It predicts withheld coding-gene expression and selected non-coding targets in total-RNA data, and recovers lineage-specific expression patterns in spatial measurements. Prediction also extends across species, including to targets without expression-pretraining exposure. Its gene representations retrieve known non-coding modules and developmental or neural programmes, including from sparsely detected RNA queries. Together, these results establish CellNet as a scalable framework for interpreting cellular diversity through increasingly comprehensive transcriptomic measurements.

  <summary>Details</summary>

Method: Applied a sliding-window Temporal Irreversibility Index to source-reconstructed EEG across three complementary cohorts (n = 141), decomposing hierarchical brain trajectories into three orthogonal temporal descriptors. A joint Hidden Markov Model was used to link dynamical shifts to microstate substrates.

Cumulative meditation practice drove a universal, pan-cerebral reduction in exploratory breadth of hierarchical dynamics, tracking dose-dependently with lifetime practice hours. Dynamical shifts linked to distinct microstate substrates, and longitudinal changes selectively tracked improvement in attentional presence in daily life.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Explicit network-dynamics modeling of spiking neurons is complex; the authors seek an ensemble-level, statistical-mechanical description of entire spike histories to capture macroscopic regimes and their transitions more tractably.

Method: Construct an ensemble over complete spike trains, assigning each an effective energy functional measuring consistency with synaptic interactions, yielding a Boltzmann-like distribution where effective temperature reflects stochastic external drive. Macroscopic states are described by coarse-grained observables (e.g., population firing-rate densities) from energy-entropy competition. The oscillatory/non-oscillatory transition is diagnosed with order parameters (rate-density entropy, temporal localization, autocorrelation). Monte Carlo simulations using Metropolis-Hastings on spike trains complement the analysis.

Result: Identified distinct regimes: non-oscillatory states include broadly uniform and temporally localized collapsed states, while oscillatory states show repeated activity peaks. Simulations reproduce predicted macroscopic regimes and provide sampling-based probes of the free-energy landscape.

Conclusion: A spike-train-level statistical-mechanical ensemble offers a tractable, order-parameter-based account of neuronal collective dynamics and oscillatory transitions, validated by direct sampling.

Abstract: We introduce a statistical-mechanical framework for spiking neuronal systems in which entire spike trains, defined as spatiotemporal configurations of discrete events, are treated as microstates of the system. Instead of modeling explicit network dynamics, we construct an ensemble over complete spike histories and assign to each configuration an effective energy functional that quantifies its consistency with intrinsic synaptic interactions.

Macroscopic states are described in terms of coarse-grained observables, such as population firing-rate densities, and arise from a competition between energetic contributions favoring interaction-consistent spike patterns and entropic contributions favoring spatio-temporal disorder.

The simulations reveal the emergence of the predicted macroscopic regimes and provide a direct sampling-based probe of the free-energy landscape.

  <summary>Details</summary>

During wakefulness, activity progressively degrades the very substrate it emerges from, causing parameter drift (gain, threshold, integration time) and reduced computational precision. Existing sleep-need explanations invoke homeostatic regulation of unknown physiological variables.

Method: A theoretical perspective arguing from non-equilibrium thermodynamics: framing brain function as a dissipative process in which activity drives entropic drift of the structure-function coupling, and positing sleep as the recalibration phase counterbalancing this drift.

Abstract: Biological function operates far from thermodynamic equilibrium, sustained by metastable structures that continuously cycle through synthesis, function execution, and degradation--consuming energy and exporting entropy in the process. Neural computation exemplifies this dissipative organization, relying on low-entropy structures engaged in dynamic processes that define key information transfer and processing parameters such as gain, threshold, and integration time. This perspective proposes that sleep serves as a systemic recalibration phase required to reset crucial signaling parameters that drift during dissipative processing. During wakefulness, neural activity progressively consumes and disrupts the organization of the very substrate from which it emerges, leading to parameter drift and reduced computational precision. Sleep maintains computational accuracy across activity cycles by recalibrating function-structure coupling--that is, by counterbalancing entropic drift. Rather than explaining the experimentally observed sleep need through homeostatic regulation of unknown physiological variables, this perspective reframes it as an emergent property of neural computation under thermodynamic constraints.
