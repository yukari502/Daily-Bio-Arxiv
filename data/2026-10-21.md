### [1] [Turning-Point Impact Compromises the Biomechanical Integrity of Cortical Bone during Oscillating Sawing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28234)
*Han Wang,Urara Satake,Toshiyuki Enomoto*

Main category: q-bio.TO

TL;DR: Turning-point impacts in oscillating bone sawing cause surface cracks and reduce local compressive strength of cortical bone; an optimized saw trajectory suppresses these impacts and preserves biomechanical integrity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Oscillating saws are standard in total knee arthroplasty, where bone surface integrity governs implant stability. The reciprocating motion produces transient impact events near trajectory turning points that may damage the prepared surface, but the biomechanical consequences had not been characterized.

Method: Oscillating sawing experiments on bovine cortical bone comparing a conventional trajectory versus an optimized trajectory designed to suppress turning-point impacts. Dynamic sawing forces quantified impact events; SEM and optical imaging characterized groove wall surface damage; compression tests assessed local and global mechanical responses.

Result: Conventional trajectory caused periodic force spikes, extensive surface cracks, and breakout regions with reduced local compressive strength. The optimized trajectory significantly lowered peak impact forces, produced smoother groove walls with fewer defects, increased local compressive strength (p < 0.001) and global compressive strength (p = 0.001), while elastic modulus was largely unchanged.

Conclusion: Turning-point impacts compromise cortical bone integrity by inducing surface damage and lowering load-bearing capacity; suppressing these transient impacts preserves the mechanical integrity of surgically prepared bone surfaces.

Abstract: Purpose Oscillating bone sawing is widely used in orthopedic procedures such as total knee arthroplasty, where preservation of the mechanical integrity of the prepared bone surface is essential for implant stability. However, the reciprocating motion of oscillating saws can generate transient impact events near trajectory turning points, potentially inducing surface damage and reducing local load-bearing capacity. This study aimed to investigate the biomechanical consequences of turning-point impact loading during oscillating bone sawing. Methods Oscillating sawing experiments were performed on bovine cortical bone using a conventional trajectory and an optimized trajectory designed to suppress turning-point impacts. Dynamic sawing forces were measured to quantify impact events. Surface damage on the groove wall surface was characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and optical imaging. Local and global mechanical responses were evaluated using compression tests. Results The conventional trajectory produced pronounced periodic force spikes and generated extensive surface cracks and breakout regions. These impact-dominated events were associated with reduced local compressive strength of the prepared bone surface. In contrast, the optimized trajectory significantly suppressed peak impact forces, resulting in smoother groove wall surfaces with fewer defects. Consequently, the optimized trajectory increased the local compressive strength (p < 0.001) and improved the global compressive strength of the specimens (p = 0.001), while the elastic modulus remained largely unchanged. Conclusion Turning-point impacts during oscillating bone sawing compromise the biomechanical integrity of cortical bone by promoting surface damage and reducing local load-bearing capacity. Suppressing these transient impacts helps preserve the mechanical integrity of surgically prepared bone surfaces.

Abstract: Two conjectures of Allman, Long and Rhodes are proven. The ultrametric exchangeable model captures the symmetries in allele distributions of ultrametric mechanistic models of coalescence and substitution in mathematical phylogeny. The dimension of this model with respect to the species tree leaf count is shown to be greatest for caterpillar trees and least for balanced trees.

Abstract: A challenge in exploring the psychology of human uncertainty is its measurement and quantification. Ranks were reliable and, through the theory's two-sided rank function, distinguished belief, disbelief, and suspension of judgment. As a clear account of human belief dynamics with metrics that can be reliably measured, Ranking Theory provides a valuable framework for investigating and contrasting probabilistic and non-probabilistic accounts of human belief.
