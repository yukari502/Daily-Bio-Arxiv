### [1] [A Multiscale Kinetic Model of DNA Damage Signalling and Repair under Combined Proton and Nanoparticle Exposure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29157)
*Mushtaq Loan*

Main category: q-bio.SC

TL;DR: A multiscale kinetic model predicts DNA double-strand break induction and repair in fibroblasts exposed to proton irradiation combined with 15-nm gold nanoparticles, using independent residual-damage data for out-of-sample validation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To connect physical radiation parameters (proton dose, LET, GNP localization, secondary electrons/reactive species) with biological DSB processing, and to explain which factors drive persistent damage under high-LET, GNP-enhanced irradiation.

Method: Couples proton dose/LET and perinuclear GNP localization to secondary-electron and reactive-species production, Poisson-Gamma-distributed lesion induction, split into simple/complex DSBs, then an ATM-γH2AX-53BP1 signalling cascade, cell-cycle-dependent repair pathway allocation (cNHEJ, HR, alt-EJ, SSA) and class-specific repair fidelities.

Result: The initial complex-lesion fraction and its slower resolution—not post-irradiation conversion of simple DSBs—are the principal determinants of persistent damage. Residual-DSB predictions show strong internal out-of-sample validation.

Conclusion: Opposing biases in correct repair and misrepair reveal inconsistency between class-specific fidelities and the supplied integrated-fidelity endpoint; repair-fate predictions are model-constrained estimates pending independent measurements of end joining, mutation, aberrations, and clonogenic survival.

Abstract: We present a multiscale kinetic framework for predicting the induction, processing, and biological consequences of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) in AG1522 human fibroblasts exposed to combined proton and 15-nm GNP irradiation. The model connects proton dose and linear energy transfer (LET), perinuclear GNP localization, secondary-electron and reactive-species production, and Poisson--Gamma-distributed lesion induction. Initial DSBs are separated into simple and complex classes and coupled to an ATM--$γ$H2AX--53BP1 signalling cascade, cell-cycle-dependent allocation among canonical non-homologous end joining, homologous recombination, alternative end joining, and single-strand annealing, and class-specific repair fidelities. The model identifies the initial complex-lesion fraction and its slower resolution, rather than post-irradiation conversion of simple DSBs, as the principal determinants of persistent damage under high-LET, GNP-enhanced irradiation. The residual-DSB results provide strong internal out-of-sample validation of the kinetic component. However, the opposing biases in correct repair and misrepair reveal an inconsistency between the class-specific fidelities and the supplied integrated-fidelity endpoint. These repair-fate predictions should therefore be regarded as model-constrained estimates pending independent measurements of correct-end joining, mutation, chromosome aberrations, and clonogenic survival.

### [3] [Learning Epidemic Threshold Equations from Simulation: REEBLRA, an Interpretable Bayesian Classification Framework for Reproduction Numbers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29399)
*Jomar F. Rabajante,Norvin P. Bansilan,Maria Czarina T. Lagura-Arreza,Destiny SM. Lutero,Emerson R. Rico*

Main category: q-bio.QM

TL;DR: REEBLRA learns interpretable surrogate equations for the basic reproduction number R0 by estimating Pr{R0(θ)>c} with Bayesian logistic regression on standardized polynomial-like features, yielding threshold brackets, uncertainty, and a mechanistic-surrogate concordance principle.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Analytic expressions for R0 are hard to derive for high-dimensional, nonlinear, or structurally complex epidemic models, so a data-driven yet interpretable way to recover epidemic thresholds is needed.

Method: Bayesian logistic regression (REEBLRA) on parameter-response data: instead of regressing R0 directly, it estimates Pr{R0(θ)>c} for thresholds c using standardized polynomial-like features and proper weakly informative priors, giving finite coefficients under complete/quasi-complete separation. A binary equation classifies invasion (R0>1) and successive threshold equations define ordered brackets approximating R0. A mechanistic-surrogate concordance principle links the fitted-score gradient to the local gradient of log R0 under regularity and tangency assumptions.

Result: On seven analytic-label benchmarks from ODE models, including a 15-compartment five-group SIR system, five-fold binary AUC was 0.999-1.000 and ordered-bracket accuracy 0.951-0.998.

Conclusion: REEBLRA complements rather than replaces mechanistic derivations, bridging simulation, ML, and reproduction-number analysis; it supports parameter screening, sensitivity interpretation, threshold classification, and exploration, with a Shiny app for implementation.

Abstract: The basic reproduction number, $R_0$, is central to infectious-disease modelling, yet analytic expressions can be difficult to derive for high-dimensional, nonlinear, or structurally complex models. We introduce REEBLRA ($R_0$ Equation Estimation using Bayesian Logistic Regression Algorithm), an interpretable framework that learns explicit surrogate equations for epidemic thresholds from parameter--response data. Rather than directly regressing $R_0$, REEBLRA estimates $\Pr\{R_0(\boldsymbolθ)>c\}$ for parameter vector $\boldsymbolθ$ and threshold $c$. It provides a binary equation for classifying epidemic invasion ($R_0>1$) and successive threshold equations defining ordered brackets that approximate $R_0$, the expected number of secondary infections caused by a typical case in a fully susceptible population. The resulting equations support parameter screening, sensitivity interpretation, threshold classification, and exploratory analysis when analytic derivation is cumbersome. Bayesian logistic regression using standardized polynomial-like features and proper weakly informative priors yields regularized estimates, posterior uncertainty, and finite coefficients under complete or quasi-complete separation. We establish a mechanistic--surrogate concordance principle linking the full fitted-score gradient to the local gradient of $\log R_0$ under explicit regularity and tangency assumptions, allowing conditional interpretation of threshold directions. Across seven analytic-label benchmarks associated with ODE models, including a 15-compartment five-group SIR system, five-fold binary AUC was 0.999--1.000 and ordered-bracket accuracy was 0.951--0.998. REEBLRA complements rather than replaces mechanistic derivations, bridging simulation, machine learning, and reproduction-number analysis. An accompanying Shiny app supports implementation.

### [8] [When to retire: the cultural evolution of post-reproductive oblique teaching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30135)
*Hirotaka Goto,Joshua B. Plotkin*

Main category: q-bio.PE

TL;DR: A stage-structured population model shows that oblique (non-kin) teaching by post-reproductive "retired" individuals can evolve without kin selection, but only in populations above a threshold size and mainly via cultural, not gradual genetic, evolution.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Instruction to non-kin (oblique teaching) is central in modern, institutionalized societies, yet it is puzzling how it can evolve since it lacks the kin-selection/inclusive-fitness explanation used for parental teaching.

Method: A mathematical model of stage-structured populations with a global carrying capacity: young individuals hunt and reproduce, then at some life stage "retire" to serve exclusively as teachers who raise the hunting expertise of the young. The authors analyze the evolution of the retirement rate and the resulting frequency of post-reproductive teachers.

Result: Retirement and teaching can evolve without kinship or inclusive fitness, but only in populations above a threshold size. Unlike classical adaptive dynamics, the evolutionarily stable retirement rates depend on the absolute population size of a mutant upon introduction. In many regimes, evolution produces fewer teachers than would maximize per-capita hunting returns or intrinsic birth rate. A mutant only slightly different from the resident generally cannot invade from a minority.

Conclusion: Because gradual evolution cannot establish teaching (small mutants cannot invade from rarity), the mechanism is best suited to cultural evolution, where a distinct retirement/elder-care norm can be adopted by a sub-population simultaneously. This links the account to cultural evolution theories, notably Henrich and Henrich's "information grandmother hypothesis."

Abstract: Teaching is ubiquitous across human societies. Instruction from parents to offspring is the most fundamental form of teaching, explained primarily through kin selection and inclusive fitness. Instruction to non-kin, known as oblique teaching, also plays a dominant role in modern societies, where teaching is often institutionalized. Nonetheless, it remains puzzling how or when oblique teaching can evolve. Here we address the evolution of oblique teaching by developing a mathematical model of stage-structured populations. Younger individuals engage in a useful activity, called hunting, and reproduce. At some point in life, they cease contributing directly to hunting and reproduction, and instead serve exclusively as teachers who increase the hunting expertise of the young. We refer to this transition in life stage as retirement, and we analyze the evolution of the retirement rate, and the resulting frequency of post-reproductive teachers, in a population subject to a global carrying capacity. We find that retirement and teaching can evolve without kinship or inclusive fitness, but only in populations above a threshold size. Unlike in classical adaptive dynamics, the evolutionarily stable retirement rates depend on the absolute population size of a mutant type upon introduction. In many regimes, evolution produces fewer teachers than would maximize the population's per-capita hunting returns or intrinsic birth rate. A mutant that differs only slightly from the resident type generally cannot invade from a minority, so teaching cannot emerge through gradual evolution. The mechanism we describe is therefore available to cultural evolution, where a distinct norm for retirement and elder care may be adopted by a sub-population simultaneously. Our account is thus tied closely to theories of cultural evolution, including the "information grandmother hypothesis" of Henrich and Henrich.

</details>
