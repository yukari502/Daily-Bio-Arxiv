*Terrence W. Deacon,Miguel Garcia-Valdecasas,Michael Jacob*

Method: The authors suggest a **recursive, Möbius-like loop** mechanism that connects forebrain information processing with midbrain metabolic regulation.

Result: NME proposes that mental experience is the result of the integration of neural processes through the **global redistribution of metabolic resources**, acting as a bottleneck that creates unitary experience.

Abstract: Most neuroscientific accounts of consciousness assume that conscious experience is constituted by the information conveyed between neurons. The physical-chemical details of the metabolic and physiological processes that convey this information are correspondingly presumed to be relevant only insofar as they support the organization of information processes. We describe an alternative perspective that denies this presumed independence of the processing of neural information and the metabolic-physiological details of these processes. We call this alternative paradigm the theory of neuro-metabolic entanglement (NME). NME proposes that mental experience exemplifies the inseparability of the organization of neural information processes from the constraints imposed by the material-energetic properties of the neural medium itself. Central to this account is a recursive, Möbius-like loop of neural connections that entangles patterns of forebrain information processing with midbrain regulatory systems that modulate forebrain metabolism. This enables patterns of neural activity to indirectly modify the global distribution of metabolic resources underlying these information processes. It also constitutes a bottleneck that integrates diverse neural processes into an adaptively relevant unitary experience organized in terms of the global redistribution of metabolic support. Computational models of neural function cannot treat energy supply as a fixed background parameter; our proposed Möbius-like loop of neuro-metabolic entanglement offers a framework for future models in which neural activity is dynamically and recursively modulated by the network's own metabolism. Recognizing this entanglement is a first step toward dissolving the explanatory gap highlighted by the so-called "hard problem" of consciousness.

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Simulation-based inference (SBI) lacks insight into which data features drive parameter estimation, and while learned weighting mechanisms offer low-cost interpretability, it is unclear if they actually capture true feature importance.

Method: The study compared learned gate weights (independent sigmoid and competing softmax) against a retrain-based ablation benchmark using Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) to infer parameters of the Jansen-Rit neuronal model.

Result: Gate-based importance was negatively correlated with ablation-based importance (Spearman's rho ~ -0.61 for sigmoid and -0.56 for softmax), and the most important feature via ablation was often down-weighted by the gate.

Conclusion: Learned weighting mechanisms (feature gates) in simulation-based inference do not reliably reflect feature importance and are negatively correlated with ablation-based benchmarks, suggesting caution when using them for interpretability.

Abstract: Simulation-based inference (SBI) provides a powerful way to fit mechanistic models to data, but offers little insight into which information in the data shapes the inferred parameter values. It could therefore benefit from robust and inexpensive interpretability methods. Learned weighting mechanisms such as feature gates provide a potential route to low-cost interpretability, although their interpretation assumes that predictive training reveals which inputs drive performance. We examined this assumption in a computational neuroscience setting, where neural posterior estimation (NPE) is used to infer four physiological parameters of a dynamical model of interacting neuronal populations (Jansen-Rit). We tested whether the learned gate weights captured feature importance by comparing them with a retrain-based ablation benchmark, in which each feature was removed in turn and the NPE retrained. We considered two jointly trained gates: independent sigmoid weights and competing softmax weights. If these reliably reflect feature importance, they should align with the ablation-based estimates. Across nine independent training seeds, however, gate-based and ablation-based importance were negatively correlated (Spearman's rho approximately -0.61 for sigmoid gating and -0.56 for softmax gating). The feature that ablation identified as most important, total signal power, was among the features the gate down-weighted most, despite gating costing negligible predictive accuracy. These results extend a pattern previously documented for attention mechanisms in natural language processing and for explainability methods in electroencephalography (EEG) deep learning to the simulation-based inference setting, and suggest caution when interpreting learned gate weights as measures of causal feature importance in scientific machine-learning pipelines.

With arbitrary missing references and a prescribed pendant the problem is W[1]-hard in the source budget K, even on positive binary trees supplied with a strict explanation using at most K+1 sources.

The mixture inferred by the published heuristic reproduces real observations better than the configuration that generated them in 282 of 296. The phenomenon is not unobservable in practice.
