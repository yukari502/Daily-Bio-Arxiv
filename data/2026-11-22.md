### [2] [Recursive extrema extraction tracks temporal organization through progressive data reduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25957)
*Vadim Shlyonsky*

Main category: q-bio.OT

TL;DR: The paper introduces a new method called **Hierarchical Maxima-Gap Statistics (HMGS)** to analyze how temporal patterns in a sequence survive across different scales of observation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Traditional methods often use a fixed observational scale or require fitting generative models. There is a need for a scale-agnostic approach that tests if organization identified at one level persists as the data is progressively coarse-grained.

Method: 1. **Recursive Reduction:** Repeatedly extract only the local maxima from a sequence. 
2. **Re-indexing:** Discard the original time clock after each step to create a hierarchy where scales are defined by the data itself.
3. **Statistical Trajectory:** Calculate the mean and variance of gaps between these maxima, plotting them as a trajectory in a 2D plane.
4. **Comparison:** Compare the observed trajectory against a reference trajectory generated by continuous IID (Independent and Identically Distributed) signals.

Result: The 'fate' of a system (whether its structure collapses, persists, or amplifies) is determined by how its trajectory moves relative to the IID reference. Crucially, this behavior cannot be predicted by looking at only the first level of data.

Conclusion: HMGS is a robust tool for studying stochastic, chaotic, and biological systems (like neuronal spike trains). It proves that temporal signatures can be attenuated or lost through successive reductions, providing a measure of the 'depth' or 'resilience' of temporal organization.

Abstract: Temporal organization is commonly characterized at a prescribed observational scale. Here, a sequence is repeatedly reduced by retaining only the local maxima surviving the preceding extraction. After each step, retained maxima are re-indexed, discarding the original clock and generating a hierarchy whose successive scales are determined by the surviving observations. Mean and variance of gaps between local maxima define hierarchical maxima-gap statistics (HMGS), represented as a trajectory in a two-dimensional statistical plane. Continuous independent and identically distributed (IID) signals generate a distribution-independent reference trajectory, whereas the displacement of structured processes may collapse, persist or amplify relative to it. Crucially, collapse becomes visible only when the accessible hierarchy reaches the characteristic scale of the organization, coupling the required record length to the scale being tested. These fates occur across stochastic, chaotic and event-derived systems and cannot generally be inferred from the first level. HMGS complements analyses based on correlations, entropy, recurrence or prescribed scales by asking a distinct question: whether organization identified at one level remains detectable as the observations themselves define successively deeper levels, without fitting a generative model or prescribing an external coarse-graining scale. In synthetic neuronal populations, superposition creates non-IID trajectories, while source organization persists or is attenuated according to its temporal structure. For mouse population spike trains, median covariance-normalized separation from matched shuffled-source controls decreased from 9.1 times intrinsic null variability at the initial level to 1.2 times after three recursive reductions. HMGS thereby probes which signatures of temporal organization survive progressive data reduction.
