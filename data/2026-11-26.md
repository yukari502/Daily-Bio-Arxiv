*Nickolay Korabel,Tom H. Millard,Thomas Andrew Waigh*

Main category: q-bio.CB

TL;DR: The study investigates how basement membrane (BM) composition influences the movement patterns of *Drosophila* hemocytes during embryonic development using physics-informed neural networks and effective diffusivity ($D_{eff}$) analysis.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Traditional metrics of cell motion (persistence and amplitude) vary too significantly to track developmental changes or the influence of BM composition effectively.

Method: The researchers used selective plane illumination microscopy to track hemocytes in control, laminin-deficient ($LanB1$), and collagen IV-deficient embryos. They applied a physics-informed neural network to estimate persistence ($H$) and amplitude ($D$), allowing for the calculation of effective diffusivity ($D_{eff}$) to measure 'cellular cooling' and 'forgetting.'

Result: 1. **Developmental Trend**: Control cells 'cool' (decreasing $D_{eff}$) while maintaining high persistence ($H$). 2. **Laminin Deficiency**: Cells lose both $D_{eff}$ and $H$, behaving more like Brownian motion (losing memory/persistence). 3. **Collagen IV Deficiency**: Cells 'cool' but retain high persistence.

Conclusion: Hemocyte movement acts as an 'annealed search' mechanism. Laminin—not collagen IV—is the critical component responsible for maintaining the persistent, patrolling nature of these cells.

Abstract: Hemocytes, the macrophages of Drosophila, disperse through the embryo and then patrol its tissues, moving along basement membranes that they help to build. Because the persistence (H) and amplitude (D) of hemocyte motion vary along each track, it is difficult to describe how movement changes during development and how it depends on basement membrane composition. We analysed trajectories, obtained by selective plane illumination microscopy, of hemocytes in control embryos and in embryos deficient in the basement membrane components laminin (LanB1-deficient) or collagen IV (collagen-deficient). A physics-informed neural network estimates H and D in short windows, each with a calibrated uncertainty. From them we construct an effective diffusivity Deff, defined for any anomalous diffusion as the diffusivity of the unpredictable part of the motion. Its error is uncorrelated with that of H and acts as an effective temperature. Cells decelerate during development by cooling (falling Deff) and forgetting (falling H). Control cells cool gradually while staying persistent. LanB1-deficient cells cool further, forget, lose the coupling between speed and persistence and become nearly Brownian. Collagen-deficient cells cool but keep most of their persistence. Hemocyte movement thus resembles an annealed search, in which laminin, rather than collagen IV, keeps patrolling cells persistent.

In this review, we divide this spectrum into two classes: normative models start from a hypothesis about how multisensory signals should be integrated, while neural network models aim to emulate how circuits could integrate them. In this review, we provide a didactic overview of multisensory models, complete with accessible equations and Python code, and compare their assumptions and explanatory power.
